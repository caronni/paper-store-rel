# measure/mic_discovery — what a colour discovery capture exposes

Owner's ruling D5 (2026-10-09): show, per engine, what the yellow
tracking dots (MIC) and the printed HCAL self-report strip expose,
measured ONLY on the COLOUR HCAL DISCOVERY captures — never on a payload
sheet. One capture per run, read-only, no decode of anything but the
self-report strip.

    PYTHONPATH=src:. env/bin/python -m measure.mic_discovery \
        --evidence-root <evidence archive root> \
        --capture paper-store-evidence-holo-2026-10/031-xerox-altalink-discovery/discovery/captures/discovery-color-canon-4800rgb.tiff \
        --out build/mic/altalink-c8245

(2026-10-09: the example names the published archive path; the capture is published as
`.tiff.jxl`, rebuilt by the release's `tools/unpack.sh`. The results table below keeps the
names the captures were read under that day.)

Writes under `--out`: `lattice.json` (windows, page-wide lattice, fit,
repeat, walls), `cell.png` (the repeating cell, one square per lattice
site: black = dot, white = empty, grey = never observed) and
`cell-tiled.png` (two cells each way — a cell whose rows wrap with a
shift reads more easily tiled), `crop-1to1.png` (raw capture, no
resampling, centred on the densest indexed dots) and `crop-deficit.png`
(its blue deficit with gain — the dots are near invisible in the raw
crop), `decode.json` (the DocuColor attempt), `report-strip.json` (the
self-report). Wall: 21–28 s per capture on machine 1 (2400 and 4800 alike;
the band pass is 16–21 s of it). The TIFF is memory-mapped; resident
size is 3.7 GB (2400) / 9.0 GB (4800), almost all of it the mapped file.

## Method

1. **Yellow plane.** Box-decimate to ~1200 dpi in row bands; per 10 mm
   block, white-balance each channel on the paper's own white (90th
   percentile); yellow = blue deficit `d = (r + g) / 2 − b`. Threshold =
   deficit median + 8 robust sigma (floor 0.03), blobs grown at 4 sigma.
   Only paper 6 mm or more from any printed content counts (toner
   scatter beside content images the content's grid); a candidate dot
   is 0.105–0.45 mm in equivalent diameter (paper specks and the M281's
   yellow background scatter sit below the floor).
2. **Lattice.** Per 20 × 20 mm window (the four blankest and the two
   most printed): the autocorrelation of the yellow map, peaks at robust
   z > 8, reduced to a basis. Page-wide: the histogram of difference
   vectors between all candidate dots (the point-set autocorrelation),
   peaks refined to sub-bin, and the basis generated by the strong peaks
   AND their differences — on the Canon no two occupied sites are closer
   than 24 printer dots but the sites are 6 apart, so only a difference
   of two peaks shows the pitch. The page-wide basis is the one used.
3. **Sites and the cell.** Dots are indexed onto lattice sites
   breadth-first from the best-connected dot, gaps bridged by local
   affine maps (the Canon page is non-affine by more than its own site
   pitch); a dot its neighbours place more than 0.3 of the finer pitch
   off its site is dropped. A lattice is accepted only with ≥ 30 indexed
   dots sitting ≤ 0.08 mm rms from where their neighbours put them. The
   site matrix (dot / known-empty / unknown) is searched for repeat
   vectors (an occupied site's partner occupied ≥ 80 % of the time); the
   two shortest independent ones span the repeat lattice, and every
   repeat is folded onto one cell by majority vote.
4. **DocuColor attempt** (`docucolor.py`): the EFF's 2005 reading of the
   Xerox DocuColor pattern — 15 columns × 8 rows, row 1 and column 1
   parity, odd parity on every data column and row, columns 2 minute,
   5 hour, 6 day, 7 month, 8 year, 11–14 serial (two decimal digits
   each, read 14 13 12 11). Every placement on the folded cell (all
   origins, all eight orientations, site strides 1 and 2, no cell site
   visited twice, at least 10 dots in the data area) is a trial; the
   chance expectation is trials × 2 × 2⁻²¹. The column mapping is the
   guide's as recalled, never checked against a real DocuColor print.
5. **Self-report strip** (`strip.py`): no registration — rows of a
   ~600 dpi luma whose dark run spans most of a strip width are
   candidate border bars, and a window around each goes to
   `holo.characterize.report_strips.decode_report_image` (CRC-checked).

### In plain words

- **Every engine measured lays a yellow dot lattice on a colour
  discovery page, and the self-report strip never carries a serial.**
  The strip's `serial` comes from PostScript `statusdict /serialnumber`;
  all five interpreters (and ghostscript) returned nothing. What the
  strip does expose: product name, firmware version, interpreter level,
  page size, colour model, and `print_nonce` = PostScript `realtime`, a
  millisecond clock of arbitrary origin (likely time since the
  interpreter started) — not a calendar time, but it orders and spaces
  prints: the Canon's two sheets are 15.7 s apart by it.
- **The two Xeroxes share one format.** Same 24-dot (1.016 mm) square
  lattice, same 16 × 16-site payload cell repeated with a half-cell
  offset every second row of cells (a 16 × 32 rectangle holds two), the
  same 52 dots per cell. The AltaLink's cell matches the VersaLink's
  turned 90° (the sheets presumably lay differently on the glass), and
  aligned so the two agree on 212 of 256 sites (the AltaLink cell
  shuffled, best of the same 2,048 alignments: median 192, max 196 over
  30 seeded shuffles): a shared frame, a column of marker dots among it,
  and some 44 sites that differ between the two machines/prints. (A
  one-off comparison run by hand on the two `cell.png`, not part of the
  instrument.) It is NOT the 2005
  DocuColor layout: one 15 × 8 placement per cell passes all 21 parity
  checks (chance 0.002 — the shared frame makes that placement
  structural), but its fields read minute 118 and hour 54/42, which
  cannot be. (The VersaLink reading's day 17, month 9 equals its print
  date, 2026-09-17; the AltaLink's reads day 0, month 15 for 2026-08-05.
  One coincidence, not a decode.) So: serial and time are presumably in
  those ~44 differing sites, and this instrument cannot read them.
- **The Canon prints a sparse 18-dot pattern in an 8 × 8 mm cell** on a
  0.25 × 0.51 mm site grid (6 × 12 printer dots), 0.21 dots/mm². Its two
  sheets, printed 15.7 s apart (by the strip's clock) with different
  resolution requests, carry the IDENTICAL cell — so nothing in it
  changed between those prints (a minute-resolution timestamp would not
  have, unless a minute boundary fell between them; a seconds clock is
  excluded). Not a DocuColor block (too sparse to be one).
- **Both HPs show an 18-dot (0.762 mm) square lattice.** The M681's is
  plain: visible dots (blue deficit ~0.057, ~15 counts), but only on the
  left third of its page — where its yellow prints at all (e039: Y dead
  on two thirds); there the repeat is 18 × 23 sites (13.7 × 17.6 mm),
  47 dots, folding at 99.5 %. The M281's (weak-Y epoch) is statistical
  only: the page-wide pair histogram peaks at exactly the M681's 18 × 18
  dots (z 114), 1,331 dots index onto it at 0.06 mm local rms, but the
  dots are ~8 counts deep among yellow background scatter, not visible
  by eye in the crop, and too sparse for a repeat. The channel facts'
  "M281: no MIC found" (e002.0a) used a detector that required a
  28-count blue drop — dots this faint were below it, so the two
  records do not contradict; a fresh-toner colour discovery sheet would
  settle the M281. No DocuColor block on either.
- **So a published colour discovery capture exposes:** the printer
  make, model and firmware in clear (strip), an interpreter clock in
  milliseconds (strip), and on every engine but the weak-Y M281 a
  repeating dot pattern that is stable across the sheet (folds at
  99.3–99.8 %) and — on the two Xeroxes — a shared format whose data
  part differs between machines. No serial number and
  no timestamp decoded to the minute on any engine here; that is a limit
  of this decoder (only the 2005 DocuColor reading is implemented), not
  evidence that the patterns carry neither.

Single captures, one per sheet, measured once; nothing promoted.
