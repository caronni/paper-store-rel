The core idea · storage is a comms problem

What if the wire were a sheet of paper?

Stop thinking "file format." A printed archive is a link: you transmit once (print), receive much later (scan), and the medium in between is lossy, warped, and 100 years long. That reframe means every tool from digital comms transfers directly.

Why bother · the use case

The one archive that outlives its own reader

Tape, optical and disk all die the same two deaths: the bits rot, and the drive that reads them goes extinct. Paper needs only eyes-or-a-camera and the printed spec. That's the whole pitch.

  • Cold / dark archives — legal, medical, cultural, "put it in a box for 50 years" data.
  • Air-gapped & tamper-evident — offline by construction; no firmware, no network.
  • Disaster-recoverable — a torn, wet, partly-burned stack still decodes (that's layer 4).
  • Self-contained (R5) — recovery needs only the sheets, the public seed, and a spec printed alongside. No cloud, no key server, no vendor.
R1 · density

Max net bits / A4 at laser + consumer-scanner resolution.

R2 · holographic recovery

Any fraction f of the printed area rebuilds the whole payload.

R3 · no overt structure

No marks, headers, quiet zones. Every region looks like uniform noise.

R4 · longevity

Decodable after 50–100 yrs; graceful under damage & fade.

The hard corollary (R2). No scheme evades it, holograms included: net payload ≤ f × raw capacity of the whole volume. "Any half recovers everything" literally costs you half your bits. Choosing f honestly is the central design tension.
Talk track (~3 min). Lead with the reader-extinction problem — network engineers have all restored from a backup whose tape drive no longer exists. Paper's superpower is that the reader is a camera and never goes obsolete. Then the five requirements: R1 density, R2 holographic recovery, R3 no visible structure, R4 longevity, R5 self-containment. Spend real time on the R2 corollary — it's an information-theoretic conservation law, not an engineering limit. Physical volume holograms obey the same rule: a fragment reconstructs the scene at lower resolution = fewer bits. This bound reappears in layer 4 and in the single-layer discussion.

Layer 0a · the medium

Why laser toner — and why CMY, never CMYK

Fused toner is a pigment crystal encapsulated in melted polymer, keyed into the fiber — not a dye in solution. That's the difference between a museum pigment and a poster that fades in two summers. Waterproof, UV-resistant, chemically inert in dark storage.

  • Use 3 planes: C, M, Y — each detected in its complementary scanner channel with a stable 3×3 unmix in optical-density space.
  • Never K in color mode — carbon black absorbs all three bands, so a K dot is spectrally colinear with a C+M+Y stack. 4 planes → 3 measurements is rank-deficient.
  • Yellow is the weak plane — azo photochemistry, paper yellowing raises noise in exactly its detection band, plus the printer's tracking dots land there. It gets a lower code rate.
Talk track (~2.5 min). The chemistry is why paper is even viable for a century. Toner = pigment-in-resin, fused into the fiber — archivally comparable to silver-halide, better in some axes. The load-bearing decision is CMY not CMYK: it's a spectral rank-deficiency argument, not chemistry. Three scanner channels (RGB) can't cleanly separate four colorants when one of them (K) absorbs in all three. So we run C, M, Y as three independent binary planes and unmix in density (log-reflectance) space where overlaps are linear. Yellow is the designated weak link and gets unequal protection — foreshadow the per-plane LDPC rate ladder in layer 3.

Layer 0 · characterize the link before you trust it

The channel: what print-then-scan does to a bit

Per symbol the channel is binary-in, soft-out, with 2-D intersymbol interference and a piecewise-smooth warp. Three consequences fall out immediately.

  • Symbols must beat single dots — hence a 3×3-dot cell, not a dot.
  • Detection must be soft — grayscale in, log-likelihoods out; 2-D equalize.
  • Sync must track warp — smooth in fast-scan, stepped in the process direction.
A forensic win. Rotate-the-page tests split the warp by owner: the printer contributes smooth 60–80 mm wander; the scanner contributes the discrete transport steps + a fixed 18.6 mm CCD-joint comb. Attributing each to its source is what let the sync layer model them separately.

Talk track (~3 min). The rule that governs the whole project: characterize the link first. Before any archive run we print a self-describing calibration sheet (CAL-1) and measure dot gain, PSF, warp, crosstalk, per-plane BER, MIC dots — output is a parameter block that feeds the simulator. Emphasize the three consequences (big cells, soft decisions, warp-tracking sync) because they justify layers 1–3. The warp-attribution story is a nice "forensics" beat network folks enjoy — same discipline as isolating whether jitter is in the transmitter or the receiver. Numbers: cell=3 works (~2% raw, ~0.2% after a blind detector), cell=2 is the density canary that fails first on a bad day, cell=1 is dead — that's the Nyquist wall of this channel.
Layer 1 · the scrambler is the whole trick

Modulation: the dumbest constellation, on purpose

Symbol = one 3×3-dot cell, solid on or off. At this resolution the individual dot isn't a reliable channel use — the cell is. Maximum per-symbol margin, fully self-clocking.

Where did group-code recording's three jobs go? All delegated to one XOR with an AES-CTR keystream keyed by (seed, cell address):

  • DC balance — coverage pinned near target.
  • Clock content — every patch is spectrally white → strong lattice peaks.
  • Run-length limiting — no runs long enough to matter.
  • Invisibility (R3) — free — the printed field is i.i.d. Bernoulli. Statistically white at every scale.
Comms analog: the self-synchronizing scrambler in SONET / PCIe / 10GbE — same polynomial-whitening role, here doing quadruple duty at zero rate cost.
Talk track (~2.5 min). Two ideas. First: the constellation is deliberately trivial — 3×3 solid on/off — because margin beats cleverness on this channel; fancier alphabets (multilevel, constrained patterns) are characterized but the simple cell wins on robustness. Second, and this is the payoff: the scrambler. One XOR with a keyed AES-CTR stream simultaneously does DC-balance, clock content, run-length limiting, and the R3 invisibility requirement — the sheet becomes i.i.d. Bernoulli, statistically white at every scale. Network folks know this exact move: the self-sync scrambler in SONET/PCIe/10GbE. Here it's load-bearing for aesthetics and deniability too, not just DC balance, and it costs zero rate. Note the honest caveat: v0.2 also thought the scrambler gave addressing for free — it doesn't, which sets up layer 2.
Layer 2 · knowing where you are, with no landmarks

Sync & addressing without a single fiducial

Local geometry — the lattice is its own clock. Any patch, after a rectifying nonlinearity (a gradient map), shows four sharp Fourier peaks at the cell pitch. That's a 2-D squaring timing-recovery loop; it hands you scale, rotation and the warp field, and high-passes mottle away for free.

Absolute position — a hidden pilot lattice. ≈2.5% of cells are pilots carrying a 2-D window-property code: every k×k window occurs exactly once, so a clean window decodes straight to (x,y) — lookup, not search. Same math as the Anoto pen and de Bruijn tori; BCH-protected so pilots tolerate the payload's BER.

The bug they caught. v0.2 tried to correlate the field against the keystream for position. It's provably flat — every cell is data⊕PN, so the correlation sums (−1)^data ≈ 0. No processing gain. That failure is why the pilot lattice exists.
Talk track (~3 min). This is the cleverest layer and the one comms people will most enjoy. Two sub-problems: local geometry and absolute phase. Local geometry — the data lattice self-clocks: FFT any patch and you get peaks at the symbol rate, but only after a rectifying nonlinearity, because a random NRZ field has a spectral null at the symbol rate (this bit them in the very first simulator test — same reason you square a signal for timing recovery). Absolute phase — you can't correlate against the keystream (the (−1)^data cancellation, worth explaining on the board), so you spend ~2.5% of cells on a pilot lattice carrying a window-property code. That's the Anoto pen / de Bruijn torus trick: every window is globally unique, so you read coordinates instead of searching. Punchline for layer 4: position is the metadata — no headers anywhere. Grain of the hologram is set here at ~3–4 mm.
Layer 3 · the same LDPC that's in your router

Inner FEC: turn a noisy tile into a clean bit — or an erasure

Tile the page into 64×64-cell blocks (~4096 bits, ~4×4 mm). Each is decoded by a soft-decision LDPC from the DVB-S2 / 5G-NR family, taking the equalizer's LLRs. A tile that decodes and passes its interior CRC is a clean symbol; a tile that fails is declared an erasure and handed up.

  • Per-plane rate ladder — R_C = R_M = 0.75, R_Y = 0.60, R_K = 0.65. Weak plane, more protection.
  • Interleave locally only — within a tile, never across the page. Global interleave would kill the fragment-locality R2 demands; bursts are the outer layer's job.
  • Turbo equalize ⇄ decode — the cheapest +1–2 dB in the system; pushes the workable raw-BER regime toward 10⁻¹.
Job of this layer: convert a graceful analog channel into a clean digital one — "mostly-correct tiles + flagged erasures." That's exactly the input the fountain layer wants.
Talk track (~2.5 min). The mental model: layer 3 digitizes the channel. It takes soft LLRs from the equalizer and produces either a clean tile or an honest erasure — with a CRC so the failure is known, never silent. It's the same LDPC family the audience already ships in 5G-NR, Wi-Fi 6, DVB-S2 — familiar tooling. Two design choices worth defending: per-plane rate ladder (yellow gets more protection, tying back to the media slide), and local-only interleaving — this is counterintuitive to a comms person who reflexively interleaves globally for burst immunity, but global interleave would destroy the spatial locality R2 needs. Bursts (a torn corner) are deliberately left for the outer layer. Turbo EQ⇄decode is the free lunch.
Layer 4 · where "holographic" actually happens

Outer erasure: any fragment rebuilds the whole

Encode the payload with a RaptorQ fountain code (RFC 6330) across the entire multi-page volume. Each inner tile is one fountain symbol. Recovery succeeds from any K(1+ε) distinct symbols — +2 symbols ⇒ failure below 10⁻⁶ — and the geometry of the survivors is irrelevant.

  • ESI = F(plane, page, x, y) — symbol identity is computed from recovered position. No headers exist anywhere. Layer 2's coordinates are the addressing.
  • Contiguous patch = confetti = core-sample — all the same to an exchangeable code. The "3-D bite through a stack" is free.
  • Priority strata — index/spec at f≈0.02 (any few cm² gives the catalog), critical at 0.3, bulk at 0.7.
Talk track (~3 min). This is the emotional peak — the "holographic" promise cashes out here, and it's just good erasure coding. RaptorQ (RFC 6330) is exactly what the room may know from 3GPP MBMS / reliable multicast: rateless, systematic, decodes from any K(1+ε) symbols with ε tiny. Two things make it magical on paper. (1) ESI = F(plane,page,x,y): symbol identity is computed from geometry, so there are no headers — layer 2's job (recover position) IS the addressing. (2) Because fountain symbols are exchangeable, a contiguous patch, scattered confetti, and a diagonal core-sample through a 500-sheet stack are all the same to the decoder — the "3-D hologram" requirement falls out for free. Then priority strata: put the index at f≈0.02 so any scrap proves what the archive is, bulk at f≈0.7. This is also where R2's conservation law is spent deliberately.

Calibration — no marks

The PN field makes every decoded region a training sequence after the fact. An EM loop estimates PSF / gain / crosstalk from agreeing cells and converges cold — no test targets printed.

Verify-on-write

Every archive run is scanned and fully decoded before the source is trusted to it. Golden hashes ship in the L0 stratum.

Bootstrap (R5)

Two human-readable pages — format, public seed, reference decoder — travel with every volume. The single, deliberate exception to R3.

Talk track (~2.5 min). Now show it as one machine. Encode runs top-down (payload → fountain → tiles → LDPC → scramble → pilots → three raw bitmaps → printer's raw path, bypassing all driver color management). Decode runs the inverse and is fully blind: it recovers geometry, reads absolute position from pilots, descrambles, unmixes color, turbo-decodes, and hands surviving symbols to RaptorQ, finishing with a hash check. Three operational points that make it real: calibration needs no printed targets (the scrambler makes every region a post-hoc training sequence for an EM loop); verify-on-write (never trust the source to paper until a scan round-trips); and the bootstrap pages — the one place we intentionally print human-readable structure, so a future decoder needs nothing but the sheets.

Alternatives · the "true hologram" and why we didn't ship it

One giant nonlinear code — and the trilemma that kills it

The purist's design: print y = sign(Φx) — every cell a 1-bit pseudorandom projection of the entire payload. Perfectly smooth degradation, zero tile granularity, structure absent at every scale. The closest binary analog to a real volume hologram.

  • Compute is not the blocker — with structured operators (SRHT / sparse GAMP), a 200-page volume decodes in minutes for < $5.
  • Information theory is — the 1-bit GLM threshold sits at αIT ≈ 1.25 (a provable ~20% rate tax) and today's algorithms need αalg ≈ 1.49 (~33%).
  • And the failure mode is all-or-nothing — below f, the layered stack still returns every file whose tiles survived; this returns nothing.
The clean statement: capacity, cell-level grainlessness, tractable decoding — pick two. The layered stack picks capacity + tractability and pays with grain (the 4 mm tile). That trade, plus per-file graceful degradation, is why it's the default. Single-layer stays a research track (S2 + N4 progressive holography).
Talk track (~2.5 min). Give the alternative its due — it's genuinely elegant, and sophisticated audiences respect a well-argued road-not-taken. y = sign(Φx) makes every printed cell depend on all payload bits (CDMA-style superposition), which gives perfectly smooth degradation and no grain at all. The surprise: compute is affordable (structured operators, a few dollars) — the earlier "decoding is too expensive" intuition was wrong. What actually kills it is a proven information-theoretic tax (the 1-bit GLM thresholds, α_IT≈1.25 / α_alg≈1.49 → 20–33% fewer bits) plus a global all-or-nothing cliff, which is unacceptable for an archive. Land it on the trilemma: capacity / grainlessness / tractable decode — pick two. Layer count is bookkeeping; the real knob is how densely payload bits depend on printed cells.
Engineering discipline · simulate before you spend toner

How the thing is actually built & tested

The loop

stimulus → print (raw 9100) → scan → analyze → log. Every layer round-trips against a measured channel simulator before any hardware run. Toner and paper are consumables — nothing gets printed on a hunch.

Determinism

Every stimulus regenerates byte-identically; a SHA in the log gates reuse. All randomness flows from an explicit seed — unseeded RNG is a bug. One phantom-payload incident traced to a numpy dtype fork is why ground truth is never hand-retyped.

Two engines characterized

HP CLJ M281fdw (home, 600 dpi) and Xerox AltaLink C8245 (office). Same-sheet rotate-scan tests separate printer-frame from scanner-frame effects. Never mix scanner color modes for absolute scale.

Verify before concluding

A halftone-screen "hidden signal" turned out to be yellow scatter riding on cyan toner — cosmetic. Rule of the house: demand a discriminating test before writing a rule into the spec.

Talk track (~2 min). Credibility slide — how a research codebase avoids fooling itself. The rhythm is strict: simulate before toner. Takeaways

If you remember four things

01 · the reframe

A printed archive is a communication link across time. Print = TX, scan = RX, paper = channel. Build a receiver, not a file format.

02 · one XOR does four jobs

The AES-CTR scrambler is DC balance, clock, run-length limiting and the invisibility that makes the sheet look like pure noise — at zero rate cost.

03 · geometry is the metadata

A hidden window-code pilot lattice gives absolute (x,y). Fountain symbol IDs are computed from position, so there are no headers anywhere.

04 · holography = erasure coding

RaptorQ over the whole volume makes patch, confetti and core-sample equivalent. Any fraction f rebuilds everything — bounded only by payload ≤ f × capacity.

The whole system in one line: a wireless-grade receiver — line coding, pilot-based sync, LDPC, fountain — pointed at a channel made of toner, so that any surviving scrap of a 100-year-old stack decodes the archive byte-exact.
