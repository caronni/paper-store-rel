# HOLO2-MEASUREMENTS — the recorded numbers (living)

Status: **PROVISIONAL.** Nothing here is qualified (DESIGN L15). Three
sections, and they measure different things: **§M-3.x** is the H3 bench
on toy workers (the orchestrator, not the numerics); **§H3bis** is the
real-capture gate record; **§HEALTH** is the standing ledger — pins for
invariants, dated rows for trends.

Sections §M-3.x below were produced by
`holo2/tests/_bench_h3.py` with the H2 toy placer and toy decoder in the
worker slots. What the numbers describe is the **orchestrator** — how a
dataflow of proposals, commits and wakes behaves — not the numerics. A
toy fire is a fixed numpy pass plus dictionary work; a real placement
fire is a BCH word decode or a correlation sweep with its own
allocation, cache and BLAS behaviour. H4/H5 re-measure everything that
depends on per-leaf cost, and this file gains their sections then.

**THE BENCH IS RETIRED (2026-08-15, H4 W1b / H4-PLAN R11).**
`holo2/tests/_bench_h3.py` is DELETED along with `solve/toys.py`, the
corpus it measured. The §M-3.x rows below stand as HISTORY — the
machine, the method and the numbers are unchanged and still readable —
but they are no longer reproducible from the tree, and by their own
"measured-on-toys" caveat they never described the receiver an operator
runs. What replaced the bench is driver-side: §M-WS's serial and banded
parallel real-sheet echoes, and the per-wave A/B instruments each H4
wave brings. `holo2/docs/measurements-h3.json` is kept beside this file
as the raw record of the retired run.

Method notes, because they change how the numbers read:

- **The bench reads the clocks; the receiver does not.** All timing is
  `time.perf_counter` in `_bench_h3.py`, outside `holo2`. The library's
  clock-free guarantee (L12) is unaffected and is pinned separately by
  `tests/holo2_e2e/test_real_e2e.py::test_a_real_run_reads_no_clocks`
  (it was `test_spine_e2e`'s until H4 W1b retired the toy spine).
- **`solve_s` excludes session assembly.** Ingest + survey of the two
  captures is a constant **2.87–2.93 s** per run and is timed separately
  (`assembly_s` in the JSON); it is carried-leaf cost with nothing to do
  with scheduling, and including it would flatten every ratio here.
- **`commit_s` is the serial point measured from outside it**, split into
  `transaction_s` (adjudicate + apply + trace) and `snapshot_s`
  (envelope serialization). `snapshot_s ⊂ commit_s`.
- **Every measurement except M-3.4 snapshots once, at close.** M-3.4 is
  what showed why: at a per-commit cadence the snapshot is 98% of the
  wall, and every other row would have measured the JSON serializer.
- **`fsync=False` everywhere except the two M-3.4 rows that say
  otherwise.**

## M-3.1 — free vs pinned-trace replay

| run | solve s | commits | certificate set | envelope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| free, `--workers 8` | 4.613 | 2,121 recorded | `f1b6a7ff…` | reference |
| pinned-trace replay | 11.574 | same ordering, verified position by position | `f1b6a7ff…` | **byte-identical** |

- Certificate sets identical, payloads identical, **envelope bytes
  identical** — records, per-key versions, spend ledger, subscriptions
  and `commit_seq` all reproduced.
- Replay costs **2.51x** the free run's wall. That is expected and not a
  regression: replay executes serially by construction and additionally
  holds proposals in a pending map until the recorded ordering asks for
  them, re-deriving the ones whose frontier moved. It is a debug and
  qualification instrument (§10.3), not an operating mode.
- Divergence is loud: every commit is checked against the position it
  occupied in the recorded run, and an ordering that cannot be followed
  raises `ReplayError` rather than producing some other state
  (`tests/holo2_e2e/test_pool_e2e.py`).

**Replay earned its keep before it was ever used as a debugger.** Getting
a threaded run to replay serially found **two real read-set defects** in
the toy placer — both of them holes in per-item determinism (L11) that
the H2 single-capture spine could not expose:

1. the placer consulted the §6 done gate over its whole extent but named
   only the tiles it *addresses*; a certificate on a tile it merely clips
   changed its verdict with nothing in its identity moving;
2. it named sibling windows on its **own footing only**, while the done
   projection is taken over every committed pose in the *region* — which
   both footings of a capture share.

Both are fixed (`toys.py`, `_reach_tile_keys` / `_siblings`). The
mechanism generalises: a read-set that under-names its inputs is
invisible to a serial run and fatal to a parallel one, and the trace is
how you find out. H4's real placer needs the done-summary resource OR-7
asks for so that this dependency is named without needing a pose.

## M-3.2 — wake amplification vs read-set breadth (OR-7)

The placer's spatial subscription swept from "every window in the region"
down to a bucket measured in window strides. `--workers 8` throughout.

| placement read-set | keys/worker (mean / max) | commits | wakes | wakes/commit | fires | useful-rerun | stale | solve s | certs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| region (H2 default) | 69.24 / 76 | 2,119 | 3,686 | 1.739 | 2,118 | 0.094 | 154 | 4.719 | 100 |
| bucket r = 4 strides | 69.24 / 76 | 2,119 | 3,686 | 1.739 | 2,118 | 0.094 | 154 | 4.506 | 100 |
| bucket r = 2 strides | 46.25 / 76 | 1,464 | 2,769 | 1.891 | 1,463 | 0.137 | 102 | 3.357 | 100 |
| bucket r = 1 stride | 31.80 / 44 | 1,156 | 2,154 | 1.863 | 1,155 | 0.173 | 53 | 2.410 | 100 |
| bucket r = 0 strides | 21.24 / 28 | 1,085 | 1,995 | 1.839 | 1,084 | 0.185 | 38 | 2.194 | 100 |

- **r = 4 is the whole region on this corpus** (5 window starts per axis),
  which is why its row is identical — the sweep saturates, it does not
  plateau.
- Wake amplification per commit barely moves (1.74 → 1.84). The cost is
  not wakes-per-commit; it is **how many commits exist at all**, because
  a broad read-set re-arms a worker that then proposes something
  identical. The diagnostic that shows it is the **useful-rerun ratio**:
  at region scope only **9.4%** of fires changed any durable fact, at
  r = 1 **17.3%**.
- Halving the read-set halves the session: region → r = 1 is −45% commits
  and **−49% wall**, with the certificate set unchanged and the payload
  still byte-exact.
- Stale in-flight discards also fall (154 → 53): a narrower read-set is
  less likely to be invalidated by a round-mate.

**Caveat that must not be lost.** OR-7 names the bucket for *hint*
subscriptions (chain transport). What this measures is the *done-gate /
sibling* subscription, which is a different dependency with a different
radius: §8.1 derives chain interference at ~6–15 window strides from warp
drift against the ±3-cell chain gate, and that derivation is not touched
by anything here. What transfers is the **direction and the shape** of
the cost — read-set breadth is paid in re-armed workers, superlinearly in
session wall — not the number.

## M-3.3 — worker scaling (OR-8)

`burn` is the toy's GIL-releasing work knob, counted in iterations (one
iteration ≈ one 65,536-element `np.exp` pass). Snapshot at close only.

| burn | workers | solve s | speedup | ms per fire | fires | rounds | mean round width | stale | stale % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | 10.957 | 1.00 | 3.56 | 3,080 | 0 (serial path) | — | 0 | 0.0% |
| 0 | 2 | 7.664 | 1.43 | 2.91 | 2,636 | 1,333 | 2.00 | 29 | 1.1% |
| 0 | 4 | 5.848 | 1.87 | 2.42 | 2,418 | 624 | 3.99 | 74 | 3.0% |
| 0 | 8 | 4.562 | 2.40 | 2.15 | 2,118 | 286 | 7.94 | 154 | 6.8% |
| 0 | 12 | 4.340 | 2.53 | 2.10 | 2,065 | 196 | 11.82 | 252 | 10.9% |
| 16 | 1 | 19.803 | 1.00 | 6.43 | 3,080 | 0 | — | 0 | 0.0% |
| 16 | 2 | 11.655 | 1.70 | 4.42 | 2,636 | 1,333 | 2.00 | 29 | 1.1% |
| 16 | 4 | 7.751 | 2.56 | 3.21 | 2,418 | 624 | 3.99 | 74 | 3.0% |
| 16 | 8 | 5.801 | 3.41 | 2.74 | 2,118 | 286 | 7.94 | 154 | 6.8% |
| 16 | 12 | 5.604 | 3.53 | 2.71 | 2,065 | 196 | 11.82 | 252 | 10.9% |
| 64 | 1 | 45.437 | 1.00 | 14.75 | 3,080 | 0 | — | 0 | 0.0% |
| 64 | 2 | 22.938 | 1.98 | 8.70 | 2,636 | 1,333 | 2.00 | 29 | 1.1% |
| 64 | 4 | 13.046 | 3.48 | 5.40 | 2,418 | 624 | 3.99 | 74 | 3.0% |
| 64 | 8 | **8.626** | **5.27** | 4.07 | 2,118 | 286 | 7.94 | 154 | 6.8% |
| 64 | 12 | 8.703 | 5.22 | 4.21 | 2,065 | 196 | 11.82 | 252 | 10.9% |

- **The knee is at 8** on a 10-core machine; 12 is flat to slightly worse
  and pays 10.9% stale discards for it.
- **The speedup is two effects, and honesty requires splitting them.**
  Rounds are formed by draining up to N ready workers, so a wider round
  absorbs more commits before anything re-arms: `--workers 1` does
  **3,080** fires where `--workers 12` does **2,065** (−33%). Normalising
  by fires, burn=64 goes 14.75 → 4.07 ms/fire, a **3.62x** per-unit-work
  speedup against the 5.27x wall figure. Both numbers are real; the wall
  one is what an operator feels, the per-fire one is what the threads
  actually bought.
- **Round width tracks the pool exactly** (2.00 / 3.99 / 7.94 / 11.82 for
  N = 2/4/8/12), so the ready queue is deep enough to keep the pool fed on
  this corpus — the scaling limit is not queue starvation.
- **Waste is bounded and small.** Stale discards rise with width, as they
  must (a wider round has more chances for a round-mate to move your
  frontier), but stay at ~11% at N = 12. No discard is an error: the
  worker re-arms and the certificate set is unchanged at every N.
- **The serial floor is the ARM step, not the commit.** Transaction time
  *falls* with more workers (0.578 → 0.243 s at burn=64) because there
  are fewer commits; what stays serial is `read_keys` + identity binding
  + the staleness re-derivation on the main thread. That is the Amdahl
  term behind the burn=0 curve topping out at 2.5x.
- **Threads, not processes.** The burn scales because a numpy ufunc
  releases the GIL; the same reasoning applies to the real leaves
  (BLAS/FFT/scipy calls all release it), and threads keep the store,
  blob mmaps and the commit layer in one address space. A process pool
  would have to serialize proposals and re-map bulk values for a
  parallel fraction the arm step already caps. **Not measured here:** a
  process pool was not built, so this is a reasoned recommendation on
  thread evidence, not a measured comparison — H4 re-opens it if a real
  leaf turns out to hold the GIL.

## M-3.4 — commit and snapshot overhead (OR-6)

`--workers 8`, one corpus, cadence swept. Envelope size at close:
**8,045,659 bytes**.

| cadence | fsync | snapshots | snapshot s | ms per snapshot | transaction s | solve s | snapshot % | transaction % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| every commit | no | 2,119 | 270.956 | 127.9 | 0.689 | 276.146 | 98.1% | 0.3% |
| every 8 | no | 266 | 36.291 | 136.4 | 0.422 | 41.220 | 88.0% | 1.0% |
| every 64 | no | 34 | 4.806 | 141.4 | 0.321 | 9.493 | 50.6% | 3.4% |
| every 512 | no | 5 | 0.883 | 176.7 | 0.313 | 5.560 | 15.9% | 5.6% |
| at close only | no | 1 | 0.263 | 262.9 | 0.287 | 4.862 | 5.4% | 5.9% |
| every 64 | **yes** | 34 | 4.974 | 146.3 | 0.332 | 9.995 | 49.8% | 3.3% |
| at close only | **yes** | 1 | 0.278 | 278.4 | 0.335 | 5.271 | 5.3% | 6.3% |

- **The transaction meets the <5% target; the snapshot destroys it.**
  Adjudication + store apply + trace append costs **0.29–0.69 s** for
  ~2,120 commits (≈0.14 ms/commit) — under 5% of solve at every cadence
  but the fastest one, where it is 5.9% only because the denominator
  shrank. There is no commit-layer problem here.
- **The snapshot is a whole-envelope rewrite and is priced accordingly.**
  128–279 ms per snapshot for 8.05 MB of canonical JSON, paid in full
  every time regardless of how little changed. Per-commit snapshotting is
  **57x** the close-only wall on a 100-tile corpus, and the cost is
  O(commits x envelope) — it grows on both axes with page size.
- **fsync is not the expensive part**: +3.5% at cadence 64, +5.7% at
  close-only, i.e. ~5 ms per snapshot. Durability here is nearly free.
- **No cadence changes the answer.** Certificates, payload and counters
  are identical in all seven rows — cadence is pure cost accounting, as
  L10 says it must be.

## M-3.6 — journal-authoritative persistence (F-16, 2026-08-13)

The F-16 build's own numbers, all at REAL-SHEET scale. Method, stated
plainly because it is not a fresh decode: the archived real state
`scratch/holo2-demo/state-c2bw-waveb` (the e029-class c2-r0700 sheet:
475.9 MB envelope, 6,105 records, 2,251 spend rows, 2,251 subscriptions,
68 MB sidecar) was re-expressed in the F-16 layout using its OWN trace,
which carries the true commit ordering AND the true per-commit
changed-key sets. (That state was trashed 2026-08-23 with
`scratch/holo2-demo/`; a fresh decode would not reproduce its commit
ordering, so this table is historical — `measure/H4-measurements/
README.md`.) Real in this table: the 3,408 batch ids, the commit
count, the per-window dirty record sets, every record value, every spend
row, the session identity, the family manifest. Reconstructed: WHICH
spend row moved in which 512-commit window (the old envelope kept only
final rows) — taken round-robin over the real rows, so the row SIZES are
the real ones. A fresh real-sheet decode is the integrator's gate; this
is the size and time law the build was measured against.

| quantity | before (envelope) | after (F-16) | target |
|---|---|---|---|
| durable record per sheet | — (17 MB trace, instrumentation) | **journal 217.3 KB** (65.3 B/commit x 3,408) | hundreds of KB |
| state on disk | **475.9 MB** | **cache 20.1 MB** (7 frames, 1.1 → 20.1 MB cumulative) | 10–30 MB |
| write at the cadence | 4.7 s (whole-envelope rewrite) | **28 ms median frame, 801 ms max** | ≪ 4.7 s |
| load of that state | 9.9 s | **2.82 s cold, 1.68 s warm** (journal read 3 ms; cache fold 1.56 s) | ≪ 9.9 s |
| per-commit journal fsync | n/a (durability was the snapshot's) | **0.053 ms/commit → 0.18 s over the sheet** | reported |

- **The facts were never the problem.** 6.6 MB of records against 476 MB
  of envelope: 147.5 MB was spend identities and 143.4 MB the same keys
  again as subscriptions (this sheet: 237.8 MB + 231.9 MB). Interning the
  keys and deriving the subscriptions is where the 23.7x comes from, not
  from any compression.
- **Subscriptions are derivable, measured before they were deleted**:
  2,251 spend rows, 2,251 subscriptions, identical worker sets, identical
  key sets, one route per worker, ZERO differences on this sheet. The one
  writer that parks a read-set without an attempt (a re-offerable
  decline) rides a per-frame override row; every row on this sheet is
  `null`.
- **The max frame is the honest number to quote alongside the median**:
  frames carrying the wide payload read-sets cost 801 ms, the small ones
  ~28 ms. Both are against a 4.7 s rewrite that was paid EVERY time.
- **fsync per commit is affordable** — 0.053 ms on this filesystem, 0.18 s
  over a whole sheet — which is what makes the journal the durable record
  instead of another thing that lags.

## M-3.5 — eager vs phased payload (OR-5)

Same corpus, same pool (8), same burn (64); the only difference is the
recipe constant — `RECIPE_V0` (payload eager, gate `always`) versus
`RECIPE_V0_PHASED` (payload below both placement entries, gate
`quiescent`). Two recipes, not two code paths.

| recipe | solve s | fires | commits | rounds | place fires | payload fires | certs | payload |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| eager (`RECIPE_V0`) | **10.372** | 2,118 | 2,119 | 286 | 2,018 | 100 | 100 | byte-exact |
| phased (`RECIPE_V0_PHASED`) | 20.686 | 4,578 | 4,579 | 607 | 4,478 | 100 | 100 | byte-exact |

- **Identical certificate sets, identical payload, identical BP-attempt
  count** (100 payload fires either way — every work key decoded on its
  first armed attempt in both schedules).
- **Phasing costs 2.0x wall and 2.2x placement fires for nothing.** The
  mechanism is the §6 done gate: eager payload mints certificates while
  placement is still running, and certificates are what retire placement
  work (`done_covered` fires 21 eager vs 42 phased, and 2,018 → 4,478
  placement fires). A placement→payload barrier withholds exactly the
  facts that make placement stop.
- This is the eager-by-default ruling (§8.3) confirmed in the direction
  DESIGN predicted, and by a mechanism DESIGN already names — measured on
  toys, so the magnitude is not transferable, but the sign is structural.
- **The other half of OR-5 is not answerable here.** A per-key attempt
  ceiling under re-arming frontiers is about a decoder that *fails* and
  is retried; the toy decoder either decides every bit or does not, so it
  never exercises a BP budget. H4/H5 owe that number.

Findings, each discriminated rather than argued:

1. **Per-fire cost scales ~linearly with concurrent workers**
   (1.0 → 7.7 → 26.9 → ~40 ms) — GIL convoying on the H3bis stand-in
   leaves' Python glue. [CORRECTED same day by M-WS2: the inflation is
   mostly WAITING on a starved main thread, not fire execution — the
   attribution and the acquittals live there.] Accelerate ACQUITTED (BLAS pinned to one
   thread: wall 282 vs 283 s). F-17 ACQUITTED as the wall culprit by
   direct attribution: builds did go per-commit as predicted (2,058 on
   gen-clean) but cost 27.0 s = 2.0% of busy.
2. **Serial is the fastest configuration on both fixtures.** Real
   sheet 329 s = the fastest decode of this sheet ever recorded (prior
   record 453 s, barrier w8). The whole decode is 221 s of fire work.
   The barrier's 25% utilisation had throttled effective concurrency
   to ~2.3, hiding the contention for fourteen echoes — the pool
   NEVER helped, under either engine, at any point in the campaign.
3. **OR-8's curve, measured on real leaves**: threads are
   negative-value on the current (stand-in) leaf profile — throughput
   FALLS below serial as workers rise. Standing answer: threads, at
   `--workers 1` (or 2), until H4's real kernels re-measure the curve;
   processes return only as a measured verdict. RULED 2026-08-15:
   DEFAULT_WORKERS flipped 8 -> 1 until the H4 re-measure.
4. **Stale discards are a pure concurrency artifact**: 0 serial /
   1,772 barrier-w8 / 2,199 continuous-w8; wakes/commit 23.27 → 34.91
   (continuous delivers a wake burst per commit, the barrier batched

## Recommendations (proposed to the user; nothing adopted here)

These are proposals with their evidence, per WORKPLAN H3's gate. Adoption
is a user ruling, and DESIGN §12's rulings are unchanged until one is
made.

**OR-5 — eager payload: KEEP EAGER.** Measured: identical certificates,
identical BP-attempt counts, 2.0x wall and 2.2x placement fires for the
barrier (M-3.5). The eager schedule is safe by spend-once and strictly
cheaper because early certificates feed the done gate. The per-key
attempt ceiling stays open and is owed by H4/H5, where a decoder can
actually fail.

**OR-6 — snapshot cadence and fsync: `snapshot_every = 512`,
`fsync = True`; and record that cadence alone cannot reach the <5%
target.** Measured: the transaction is ~0.14 ms/commit (<5% everywhere),
fsync is ~5 ms/snapshot (negligible), and the snapshot is 128–279 ms
because the whole 8 MB envelope is rewritten. At 512 the snapshot is
15.9% of solve and a crash costs at most 512 commits of re-armed work —
the best point on this curve, not a good one. **The real fix is not a
knob**, and since the audit of 2026-08-10 it is not delta frames either:
the envelope grew because the spend ledger stopped folding (DESIGN §15,
D1–D3), so the fold is the repair and an incremental snapshot is an
optimization to measure afterwards. Current default is
`snapshot_every = 1`, which on this corpus costs 57x.

**OR-7 — spatial bucket for subscriptions: narrow it, and measure the
chain radius separately.** Measured on the done-gate subscription: region
→ 1 window stride is −45% commits, −49% wall, useful-rerun 0.094 → 0.173,
certificate set unchanged (M-3.2). Proposal: **default the done-gate /
sibling subscription to 1 window stride**, and do NOT set the chain-hint
radius from this number — §8.1 derives that one at 6–15 strides from warp
drift, and it is a different dependency. The deeper finding is that
holo2 should not be expressing "which windows can make me done" as a
list of sibling window keys at all: a **done-summary resource per region**
names the dependency exactly, at O(1) keys, and removes both the
amplification and the read-set defects M-3.1 uncovered. Recommended as
H4 work.

**OR-8 — worker pool: threads, default 8.** Measured: 5.27x wall (3.62x
per fire) at 8 workers on a 10-core machine with a GIL-releasing load,
knee at 8, 12 flat-to-worse with 10.9% stale discards (M-3.3). The
serial residue is the arm step (read-set derivation + identity binding +
staleness re-derivation), not the commit layer. **Process pool: not
recommended, and not measured** — the parallel fraction is already capped
by a serial step that a process pool cannot help, and it would cost
proposal serialization and bulk-value re-mapping. Reopen only if a real
H4/H5 leaf is measured holding the GIL.

## Standing caveats

1. **Measured on toys.** Every wall figure is a toy fire's cost. The
   invariance results (certificate sets, envelope equality, determinism
   across pool sizes) are structural and do transfer; the ratios do not.
2. **One corpus, one machine, one run per point.** No repetitions, no
   error bars. Differences under ~10% here should not be argued about.
3. **`assembly_s` (ingest + survey, 2.87–2.93 s) is excluded from every
   `solve_s`.** A real session pays it once per capture; it is carried-leaf
   cost and it is not what H3 is about.
4. **Nothing here is a channel claim.** The corpus raster is a synthetic
   dot field with no engine, no warp and no noise.
5. **Status is PROVISIONAL** and stays so until a recorded qualification
   and a user declaration (L15).

**THE FINDING, and it is a mechanism rather than a number: decimation is
not better or worse than native — it is better exactly when the capture
is SENSOR-LIMITED and worse when it is APERTURE-LIMITED.** An area
average suppresses white sensor noise by the root of the pixels it pools
and widens the aperture (pulling in neighbouring ink — the same context
contamination arm (d) corrects). At sigma 2-8 the second term dominates
and native wins at every rung; by sigma 24 the crossover has arrived at
the middle rungs; at 48 decimation wins everywhere. **v1's 1.34x sits
squarely on the noise-limited side, and the 2400-dpi/sigma-24 cells
reproduce it almost exactly (1.36x / 1.37x)** — which is consistent with
e028's own explanation of the decimate-to-1600 doctrine: the Canon's
glass-level MTF10 ~17 lp/mm is an ~860-dpi information ceiling, so a
4800-dpi scan is ~5.6x oversampled and its surplus samples carry noise,
not signal.

So the plan's question — "v1's 1.80%-vs-2.41% answered for holo2's
sampler" — is answered **NOT as a constant**: holo2's sampler shows the
same crossover v1's did, and a single ratio is a property of the scan,
not of the receiver. Nothing here says decimate or do not decimate; it
says the ladder's coarse rungs pay on noisy captures and cost on clean
ones, which is an argument for keeping the ladder and choosing per
capture. **Owed before any doctrine**: the same pair-matched read on REAL
4800-dpi captures (H6), where the optical ceiling is a fact rather than
an authored sigma.

1. **E018's direction is reproduced, with its reason.** The ctx gain

   context signal the correction needs has partly been averaged away
   before the detector sees it. It **saturates** once the rung resolves
   the PSF (ppc 6 and 12 are within 1% of each other).
2. **The gain is not monotone in the channel — it has a maximum.** It is
   largest in the middle (17-24x at sigma 0.45), because that is where
   the context bias dominates a still-small error budget; at sigma 0.65
   the read is bad enough that the ±4-clamped additive logit cannot
   carry it back (2.2-2.6x).
3. **THE SIGN FINDING, recorded rather than tuned away: at sigma 0.35
   the fit ADOPTS and makes payload BER WORSE** (0.0063% -> 0.1024% at
   ppc 2; 0.0000% -> 0.0063% and -> 0.0121% at ppc 3 and 6), with a
   healthy held-out log-loss improvement (-0.023 to -0.046) in every
   case. There is no contradiction and no defect: `fit_context`'s gate is
   on **weighted log-loss over format-known validation sites**, and the
   quantity that matters downstream is **hard-decision BER over payload
   cells**. On an almost-error-free page those two disagree — the
   correction sharpens well-calibrated LLRs and pushes a handful of
   correct-but-marginal cells across zero. The absolute cost is tiny
   (~1e-3 of the payload) and the LDPC absorbs it; the arithmetic is
   v1's, carried and oracle-pinned, so this is a NOTE ON THE GATE'S
   SEMANTICS, not a proposal to change it. It does mean **a ctx gain
   claim must always name the population it is measured over**.

### OR-5 — the per-key BP budget under re-arming frontiers, MEASURED (2026-08-18)

**Harness: `holo2/measure/or5_or7_census.py`** (run by path; the OR-5 half).
Tree `487fb75` (W5 exit). Three SERIAL `--complete` decodes of
`verdicts/_fixtures.py`'s own builders — **channel** (c2-r0700, 8x8, the
measured e032 warp + 2% cell error, on the finest-only reading set, which
is what `conftest.run_channel` decodes), **banded** (16% cell error over
the right quarter, default cascade) and **two-capture** (the banded page
declared TWICE: same print seed, a different sensor draw — run streaming's
rescan shape on a single sheet so it can be decoded serially, which is
what makes `union` legal). Every perturbation seeded. Walls **recorded,
not attributed** (shared machine, house rule): 2.8 s / 21.3 s / 76.0 s,
101 s for the run. All three volumes VERIFIED, payload byte-exact.

**M-3.5 owed this** and said why it could not pay: *"a per-key attempt
ceiling under re-arming frontiers is about a decoder that FAILS and is
retried; the toy decoder either decides every bit or does not."* The real
chain fails, is retried, and is re-armed — so here it is.

**OBSERVABILITY FINDING, recorded first because it shapes the row.**
holo2 does NOT make the iteration count durable. `TileAttempt.n_iters` is
transient by DESIGN §5.5; the `SpendRecord` carries `outcome` and a
monotone `exposures` counter (the CRC budget L5 prices) and nothing else;
`certified_tile_record` drops it; no event carries it. **v1 does** —
`SessionCounters.{scan,union,retry150}.bp_iterations`, folded per stage.
So OR-5 is a state read on v1 and an INTERCEPTION here: the harness wraps
`solve.tiles.attempt_k_tile` and attributes each call to its work key by
wrapping `RealPayloadWorker.run` around it. **No `src` change was made,
and none is proposed by this row** — but if a ceiling is ever wired, this
gap is its first prerequisite, because nothing in the tree can read back
what a key has already spent.

**ONE ATTEMPT'S STRUCTURAL CAP IS TWICE `max_iters`**, which is not
obvious from the constants: `format.ldpc.decode` runs the normalized
min-sum pass and then, on syndrome failure, ONE exact sum-product retry
from the channel LLRs, and `n_iters` is the sum over both. So the caps are
**120** (`BASELINE_BP_MAX_ITERS` 60) and **300** (`RETRY_BP_MAX_ITERS`
150), and a run that reports 300 is at its cap, not over it.

PER ATTEMPT, pooled over the three arms — 1,248 attempts, of which
**1,115 ran BP** and 133 refused below the attempt floor (no decoder, no
iterations):

| route | n | max | p99 | median | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `scan` | 641 | 120 | 120 | 120 | 91.2 |
| `union` | 88 | 120 | 120 | 120 | **120.0** |
| `retry150` | 386 | 300 | 300 | 300 | **299.2** |
| all | 1,115 | 300 | 300 | 120 | 165.5 |
| — of which CERTIFIED | 160 | **17** | 17 | **2** | 3.9 |
| — of which FAILED | 955 | 300 | 300 | 120 | 192.6 |

**The two populations do not overlap.** A certificate is nearly free —
median 2 iterations, never more than 17 in this corpus — because a tile
whose evidence is good converges almost immediately. Everything expensive
is a failure running to its cap: every `union` attempt and essentially
every `retry150` attempt spent its whole budget (means 120.0 and 299.2
against caps of 120 and 300) and minted nothing.

PER (ARM, KEY) LIFETIME — the sum over every BP attempt one work key ever
made. Keyed on the ARM as well as the key, deliberately: all three arms
decode the same page, so pooling `tile/0/2/6/main` across them would sum
three independent lifetimes into a number no key ever spent. (The
harness's first cut did exactly that and produced a pooled "lifetime" of

generic: a per-subject roll over arms that share a subject space needs
the arm in its key.)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| pooled | 192 | 11,820 | 5 | 961.1 | 61 | 33 of 192 |

**THE MECHANISM, and it is the whole finding: nothing bounds re-arming.**
A payload key is re-armed whenever its evidence collection gains a piece
or its `baselined` set grows, and because the read-set IS the identity
(§7), each re-arm is a NEW identity for the same (route, scope) — so
spend-once does not cap it. The two-capture arm's worst key ran
`scan x29 + retry150 x25 + union x7 = 61 BP attempts` for 11,820
iterations and **never certified**. Its scope count is 4 footings
(2 captures x 2 rungs) + 2 union ppc tags, so ONE walk of its whole ladder
is `4x120 + 4x300 + 2x120 = 1,920` iterations — the observed lifetime is
**6.2x one walk**, and that multiplier is the re-arming, measured.

Independent cross-check, same banded page, v1's own durable ledger
(`holo2/measure/v1_side_by_side.py`): `scan` 80 calls / 3,872 iterations;
`retry150` 32 calls / **9,600 = exactly 32 x 300**, `crc_ok` 0. Two
receivers, two instruments, the same shape: retries run to the cap and buy
nothing.

**RECOMMENDED CEILING (proposed with its evidence; nothing adopted here,
and nothing wired — the wiring is a separate ruled change).**

The quantity to cap is **cumulative BP iterations per work key**, not
attempts: attempts differ 2.5x in price (120 vs 300), so an attempt
ceiling would be a budget denominated in the wrong unit.

*The safety argument.* The only work a ceiling can WRONGLY refuse is work
that would have certified, and every certificate in this corpus is cheap:
the heaviest certified LIFETIME observed is **548** (channel
`tile/0/5/5/main`, 2 scan + 2 retry150 attempts) and the heaviest certified
single attempt is 17. The floor the format's own algebra imposes is one
full ladder walk at the deepest reading set measured — **1,920** — and a
ceiling below that would refuse a key its own descriptors
(gate-constants-vs-construction-floors).

**Proposal: 4,096 cumulative BP iterations per work key.** It is 7.5x the
heaviest certified lifetime, 2.1x the one-walk floor, and above the entire
banded arm's worst case (3,180) — so on this corpus it refuses nothing
that certified and nothing that a single-capture cascade even reached —
while cutting the two-capture arm's worst key by **65%** (11,820 ->
4,096).

The literal "ceiling >= observed max" reading gives **16,384** (1.39x of
11,820). Recorded because it was asked for, and labeled for what it is: on
this corpus it refuses nothing at all, so it is a TRIPWIRE against a
pathological key, not a budget. The choice between the two is a ruling,
not a measurement.

**WHAT THIS ROW DOES NOT CLAIM.** Small synthetic pages (64 tiles), two
rungs, at most two captures. The observed maximum is not a population
bound and cannot be — it grows with the reading set's rung count and with
the number of captures, which is exactly why the ceiling has to be
derived from the reading set rather than fixed blind. The real sheet's own
census is owed and belongs with the echo chain.
