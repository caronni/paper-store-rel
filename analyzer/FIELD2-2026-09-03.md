RULING this implements (Germano, 2026-09-03): page-scope CONTEXT and
page-scope POSE fight the physics, because toner interaction and pose are
local — but a correctly done per-plane page FIELD does not, because
registration IS a smooth function of position with small local
deviations. So: a low-order surface plus a short-bandwidth residual,
fitted from the page's own blind evidence, used as the PRIOR of a search
rather than as an adopted pose.

### 1.1 `fit_page_field(inputs, certs, lane, stream, *, tiles, seed)`

Per tile of the page (all covered tiles by default; a deterministic
`fold_hash` subsample when `tiles` is an int; certified tiles INCLUDED —
they carry the most labels and are the best anchors):

- footprint → lane footprint → crops → `known_frame` with the tile's own
  key as the TARGET, so a certified tile is read from its neighbours'
  labels and its own furniture, never from its own answer;
- the `offset` arm's own blind 2-D search — `arms._search_offset`, the
  same function, called, not copied — on the plane's own labels;
- recorded per tile: `offset_dots_xy`, `n_labels`,
  `n_discriminating`, `committed_logloss`, `search_logloss`, the held-out
  loss `gain` over committed, the search-profile `curvature`, `weight`,
  and any `refusal`.

**The weight is the curvature, not the gain.** Gain over committed is
zero in two opposite cases — the pose is already right (an informative
zero the field must keep) and the objective is flat (noise). The
mean-minus-best of the x sweep's profile separates them: a sharp
objective has curvature whether or not its optimum sits at zero. So
`weight = n_labels × clip(curvature / 0.02 nats, 0, 1)`, and the gain is
reported rather than trusted. (The x profile only: the y sweep is
evaluated AT the winning x, so its losses all sit near the optimum.)

Then the field: a weighted 2-D polynomial of total degree ≤ 3 in
normalised page coordinates (with a degree ladder — 8 weighted tiles per
free coefficient) PLUS a support-normalised kernel-smoothed residual
(`num = smooth(w·r)`, `den = smooth(w)`, evaluated where `den > 0`) at
production's own bandwidth (`plane_field.BANDWIDTH_TILES` = 2), plus
production's own twicing pass, clamped at `plane_field.MAX_DELTA_DOTS`.
`plane_field.smooth`, `gaussian_kernel`, `clamp`, `evaluate`,
`median_magnitude_dots` and `tile_codes` are all holo3's, called.

The twicing matters: a 2-tile kernel damps a 16-tile sinusoid by ~26%,
so a field fitted without it under-reports exactly the amplitude the
AltaLink sinusoid is interesting for. Measured on the planted field,
median error 0.152 → 0.040 dots and recovered amplitude 0.816 against a
planted 0.800.

**Honest units, and production's floor.** Everything is printed dots in
the PAGE frame (DESIGN §4.4) — the analyzer's own offsets are page-frame
by construction, since `sample.read_rgb` shifts through
`level_terms[:, :2]` without the placement's rotation, so a field2 delta
must NOT be rotated (only production's deltas are).
`holo.receiver.plugins.fields.MIN_DELTA_DOTS` refuses to publish a plane whose
median delta is under 0.25 dots, because production ADOPTS a published
field blindly (`plane_k.field_delta_dots`: the standing fit speaks first
and `choose_offset` does not even search). This instrument publishes
anyway and records `production_publishable` plus production's own
refusal string beside the number — here the field is the PRIOR of a
search still scored on held-out labels, not an adopted pose, so a
0.15-dot field costs nothing when it is wrong. The distinction is what
the arm does with it, not what the number is.

Memoised per (lane, stream, certificate token, tile sample, seed, mode,
halo, bandwidth), with a per-key lock so a threaded pass fits once.

### 1.2 The two arms

`field2` = committed + the page field's per-cell delta at the tile
(through `_override_for_delta`, the same per-owner affine machinery the
`field` arm uses); `field2+offset` = that delta as the centre of the
offset search, narrowed to ±1 dot on x (`NARROW_X_MIN/MAX`) because the
prior has already supplied the centre and a wide search on top of a
prior is the prior measured against its own freedom. Both collapse
EXPLICITLY to `committed` / `offset` with a recorded reason
(`no_field`, `no_support`, `no_observed`, a lane/page mismatch) rather
than emitting a duplicate row. Both are blind (labels only).

`_search_offset` gained two default-off keywords (`x_range`/`y_range`,
`profile`); with both off it evaluates the same grid and emits the same
row as before, so every existing arm is byte-unchanged.

The arms are reachable by name — `subtract+field2`,
`subtract+field2+offset`, `head+field2+offset` all parse — but were
deliberately NOT added to `types.DEFAULT_ARMS`: a pass-3 decode is
queued against the default battery, and widening it would change a run
in flight. Promoting them into the default battery is Germano's call.

1. **The field alone is a measured negative.** `subtract+field2` is
   worse than the default on every column at 2.5–7 SE. A page field is a
   prior, not a pose: the local deviation the search finds is worth more
   than the smooth part it replaces. (It still certifies one tile
   nothing else does, so it earns a place in a battery, not a default.)
2. **The field as the search's centre is a measured positive** on every

   mostly in the halo; R and cell error are scored on the target tile's
   own CARGO. The field helps most exactly where the tile's own labels
   cannot centre a search — which is the claim the ruling makes.
3. **C's residue is above the floor, not misregistered**: 0 of 20 in
   every arm, at median R 0.74–0.79 with the field's help. Registration
   is not what those tiles are short of.

The narrow grid was the right pre-registration: `field2+offset` moves a
median 0.25 dots from the field's centre and reaches the ±1-dot bound in
only 1.7% of lane-rows.

## 3. Gaps and owed work

- **`DEFAULT_ARMS` untouched, deliberately.** A pass-3 decode was queued
  against the default battery; widening it mid-flight would change a run
  in progress. Adding `subtract+field2+offset` (and, for the union,
  `subtract+field2`) to the default battery is Germano's ruling. The
  union number above is the evidence for it.
- **One sheet, one page, three lanes, 60 targets.** Existence and
  regression evidence, no promotion.
- **Declared deviation**: the 300 per-tile reads share ONE lane-scope
  mixing fit with no tile excluded, so for the ~0.6% of tiles that fall
  in that 12-tile sample their own certificate reaches the SPECTRAL fit
  (never the labels — `known_frame` still excludes the target key). Cost
  of doing otherwise: 300 mixing fits instead of one.
- **The y component is unvalidated.** Nothing in the record claims a
  y registration field; the two 2400 lanes agree on it to 0.04 dots but
  the 4800 lane disagrees by 0.185. Treat x as measured, y as reported.
- **Not tried**: the full-page fit (est. 192–651 s per lane-stream, so
  ~30 min for six) instead of the 300-tile sample; M (production
  published M fields only on the 1200 lanes, which no comparison here
  used); the field as a prior for `head`/`conditional` (the arm name
  `head+field2+offset` parses and is untested); pooling the three lanes'
  fields into one page field before the arms read it; and per-block
  adoption, which is what the PLANE-FIELDS W6 record says the page-scope
  adoption misfit needs.
- **The 0.02-nat curvature scale never binds on this sheet** (median
  curvature 0.28–0.34), so the weight is effectively `n_labels`. It is a
  floor against a flat objective, not a discriminator, and a sheet with
  genuinely label-starved tiles is where it would earn its keep.

# 4. The POOLED FURNITURE field (2026-09-03, late)

RULING (Germano). The per-plane registration field is a property of the
PRINTED PAGE — invariant across rungs and across captures of the same
sheet (§2.3: the two 2400 captures agreed on x to 0.065 dots, the 4800
to 0.096–0.114) and, fitted
from FURNITURE anchors only, invariant across cascade generations and
blind by construction, because no certificate enters it and so there is
no withholding hazard. Therefore ONE fit per (page, plane), pooled over
every lane; the per-lane certificate-anchored fit of §1–§2 becomes the
separate `field2c` variant.

## 4.1 What was built

| file | +/− | what |

**`fit_pooled_page_field(inputs, stream, page, *, lanes=None,
anchors="furniture", tiles=None, seed, …)`**. Every lane of the page
(`lanes_for_page`, all levels of all captures) reads the SAME tile
sample — drawn from the union of the lanes' coverage, so pooling at a
tile is over lanes and not over whichever tiles each lane happened to
draw — through `_lane_records`, i.e. exactly `fit_page_field`'s per-tile
machinery, called, with `certs = NO_CERTS`. The lane loop is threaded
(`workers`), and the result does not depend on the thread count (§4.5).

**Verified, not assumed** (both are now pins):

* `known_frame(inputs, NO_CERTS, …)` projects NO tile
  (`n_tiles_projected == 0`), its known mask is EXACTLY the furniture
  mask repeated over the three planes, and the target exclusion is moot
  — two different targets in the same tile get byte-identical frames.
  Measured on sheet H tile (20,18): 480 Y labels furniture-only against
  7,438 with certificates, 9 tiles projected.
* the offsets are already PAGE-frame (DESIGN §4.4), so no conversion:
  the pooled set mixes `rot_k` 2 (the 2400 lanes) and `rot_k` 0 (the
  4800 lanes), and if the frames differed the pooling would cancel the
  field instead of sharpening it — §4.3 shows it sharpens.

`certs` reaches the mixing fit too, so with `NO_CERTS` **the §3
"declared deviation" does not arise at all**: there is no certificate
anywhere in a furniture fit to leak through `lane_mix`'s 12-tile sample.

**Pooling rule: the component-wise WEIGHTED MEDIAN** (`wmedian`, the
default; `wmean` kept so the choice stays a measurement). A per-tile
winner is the argmin of a noisy objective on a 0.25-dot grid — §2.4
measured the winners scattering with sd 0.28–0.38 dots — and a search
that latches onto a wrong minimum is off by a whole grid step or more;
the mean lets one such lane drag the tile, the median cannot be moved by
less than half the weight. The median's own cost (it returns a value ON
the grid) is paid back SPATIALLY by the surface and the kernel residual.
Measured, and the reassuring part: **the two rules differ by a median
0.030 dots (Y) and 0.027 (C)**, p90 0.079/0.069 — the choice is not
load-bearing on this sheet. x and y are pooled separately because they
are separate physical claims. The pooled weight is the SUM of the lanes'
weights, and `n_lanes` is recorded per tile beside it.

**The memo key** is `(run-dir id + path, format, page, stream, anchors,
sorted lane ids, tile sample, seed, unmix mode, halo, bandwidth, pool
rule, token)` with **`token = None` for `anchors="furniture"`** — a
furniture fit is a pure function of the paper and the format, so the
token would protect nothing and would only force a whole-page refit
every time the cascade minted a tile, which is the cost this fit exists
to remove. The token STAYS for `anchors="certified"`, where two
certificate mappings (a `--withhold` control against the full set) must
never share an entry, and the per-lane `_memo_key` is untouched.

## 4.6 Gaps and owed work (§3 still applies except where noted)

- **The `everything` profile now carries the two pooled arms** (ruling
  item 2), so a `--profile everything` residue pass fires them. It does
  NOT touch `DEFAULT_ARMS`; that stays Germano's call, and §4.5 is not
  evidence for it.
- **The certificate count went DOWN on the 60-tile sample** (3 vs 5).
  The discriminating follow-up is the same battery over a COMPLETE
  residue rather than 60 hash-ordered tiles, where an additive arm is
  scored by union rather than by head-to-head. Estimated from phase D's
  136 s for 60 targets × 3 arms, sheet H's full Y+C residue is about an
  hour; not run here.
- **`field2_fit_workers` defaults to 1.** Inside a `fixedpoint` run the
  lazy pooled fit blocks the first target that needs it for ~6 min
  (Y, 22 lanes) while the other worker threads wait on its key lock. A
  battery run should pass `knobs={"field2_fit_workers": N}`, or the
  driver should pre-fit and hand the table in as phase D does. Wiring
  the run's own worker count through `_knobs_for` is the obvious fix and
  is NOT done (it would widen a hot path mid-flight).
- **250 tiles per lane, not the full page.** Support reached every tile
  in both planes, so the sample is not the binding constraint on
  coverage; whether 5,125 reads instead of ~43,000 costs amplitude is
  unmeasured.
- **M is still unfitted** and `head+field2+offset` still untested.
- **The pooled y component is still unvalidated** (§4.4).
- **Sheet I** would test the invariance claim where it matters most —
  the ruling's "invariant across rungs" is argued from sheet H's
  cross-capture agreement, not measured across rungs.
