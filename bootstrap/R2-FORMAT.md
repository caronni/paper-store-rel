What v1 is for: a finder photographs the page in pieces — one frame per
corner, one of the middle, closer frames of damaged parts — and feeds every
frame to the reader. Each frame is posed from the bullseyes it contains, every
block carries its own address and check, and the reader pools blocks across
frames and pages until the data is complete. Nothing on the page needs to be
in one frame.

## Geometry
- Engine grid 600 dpi. Module = 12 × 12 engine dots = 0.508 mm, 50 modules per
  inch. Ink = 1.
- Usable area 182 × 277 mm (A4, 10 mm margins, 18 mm gutter on the bound
  edge). The module grid is W × H = 358 × 511 modules (BW = 21, BH = 30 blocks;
  W = 17·BW + 1, H = 17·BH + 1), 181.9 × 259.6 mm, centred in the usable area.
- Module (c, r): column c in [0, W), row r in [0, H); its printed rectangle is
  dots [x0 + 12c, x0 + 12c + 12) × [y0 + 12r, y0 + 12r + 12).
- The bound edge is the left edge of every page (the 18 mm gutter); the
  grid's origin is x0 = 426, y0 = 441 dots (the 358 × 511 grid centred in
  the usable area). The page bitmap carries nothing but the grid; the
  leaf's overt header (volume, rung, leaf n of m), in type below the grid,
  is drawn by the leaf composer for the person sorting leaves. No reader
  reads it.

## Timing lattice
- Every row r with r % 17 == 0 and every column c with c % 17 == 0 is a timing
  module with value (r + c) % 2 — alternating along every timing line,
  consistent at the crossings. The lines bound the blocks and are the
  reader's local clock and local black/white reference.

## Blocks
- Block (bi, bj), bi in [0, 30), bj in [0, 21), occupies rows 17·bi + 1 ..
  17·bi + 16 and columns 17·bj + 1 .. 17·bj + 16 (16 × 16 = 256 modules).
- In a data block the bits are read row-major, MSB first: byte k = modules
  8k .. 8k+7 of the block's raster order.
- Fifteen blocks are bullseye blocks (next section); the other 615 are data
  slots.

## Bullseye blocks
- At (bi, bj) in {2, 14, 26} × {2, 8, 14} and {8, 20} × {5, 11, 17}: five rows
  of three, staggered, two blocks in from the top and left edges, three from
  the bottom. The four quarters of the page hold 3 to 5 whole bullseye blocks; a
  quarter-area frame placed elsewhere holds 2 to 6; a 110 × 110 mm frame
  holds at least one whole bullseye block (at least three centres); the
  vectors between neighbouring bullseyes,
  in modules, are the lattice generated by (0, 102) and (102, 51) (row, column).
- Within a bullseye block, module (r, c) in block-local coordinates 0..15,
  with dx = c − 8, dy = r − 8 and q = dx² + dy²:
  - q ≤ 42: a ring module. Ring d = the integer nearest √q; ink iff d is even.
    So ink iff q ∈ {0, 4, 5, 13, 16, 17, 18, 20, 32, 34, 36, 37, 40, 41}.
  - 43 ≤ q ≤ 56: white (ring 7, the quiet ring).
  - q ≥ 57: a header module (79 of them: the four corners of the block and its
    row 0 and column 0).
  The rings are circles of one-module pitch: along the centre row and the
  centre column the signature is thirteen one-module runs, ink at dx = 0,
  ±2, ±4, ±6 and white at ±1, ±3, ±5, framed by one white module at
  dx = ±7 (dx = −8 is a header bit; dx = +8 is the timing line, ink at the
  six bullseyes with bi + bj odd). The centre sits
  at page module (17·bj + 9, 17·bi + 9); the timing lines around the block
  are 9 modules left of and above the centre, 8 modules right of and below it.
- The header, 68 bits, in the header modules in block raster order — plain
  row-major: row 0 left to right (16 modules), then the header modules of row
  1 left to right, and so on — MSB first. The last 11 header modules are all
  of row 15: a bullseye block's bottom row is white.
    bits 0-15    length of the data in bytes, big-endian
    bits 16-47   CRC-32 of the data, big-endian
    bits 48-67   check: CRC-32 of the six bytes above, modulo 2^20
  The same header is printed in every bullseye block of every page of one
  payload. The check also tells a reader the page's orientation: no header
  reads identically in two orientations (proved over GF(2) for every turn
  and bullseye parity), and a header read in a wrong turn passes the check
  with a DIFFERENT header for about one payload in 2^20 per turn and parity
  class — permanently for that payload, since a 180° or 270° read takes
  its length field from the timing line. The GENERATOR therefore refuses a
  payload whose header passes in any wrong turn (the caller pads a byte),
  and refuses length 0 and payloads over 65,535 bytes; a reader that takes
  the turn from the timing
  frames (9 modules left of and above a centre, 8 right of and below) and
  reads the header only in that turn is immune regardless.

## Data blocks (32 bytes)
    bytes 0-1   chunk index i, big-endian, counted over the whole payload
    bytes 2-27  chunk i of the data: bytes 26i .. 26i+25, the last chunk
                zero-padded
    bytes 28-31 CRC-32 over the six header bytes (length, data CRC-32,
                as in the bullseye blocks) followed by bytes 0-27, big-endian:
                a block validates only against its own payload (ruled
                2026-10-20 night after the refuter showed frames of two
                payloads of equal length mixing)
- N = ceil(length / 26) chunks. There is no header chunk, no magic word and
  no page number: the header is in the bullseye blocks, the chunk index is
  global, and the data's CRC-32 says when the payload is complete.
- A slot that carries no chunk is white (no index, no CRC).

## Placement of the copies
- Groups and pages. Chunk j has page-local index i = j mod 204 in group
  g = j // 204; N chunks make P = ceil(N / 204) groups and P pages (the
  last group 204 or fewer). Each chunk is printed three times, copy k in
  row band k = block rows 10k .. 10k + 9 (204, 207 and 204 data slots).
  Copy k of group g goes to page

      (g + k·d) mod P,   d = max(1, (P + 1) // 3)

  (d = round(P / 3) for P = 2 .. 13; d = 1 for P = 1). So band k of page q
  holds the copies k of group (q − k·d) mod P: one group per band, every
  slot used at most once. P = 1 is the single page as it was (the same
  bitmap, byte for byte). P = 2: the home page g holds copies 0 and 2 (the
  design's farthest-apart pair, at least 19 block rows), the other page
  copy 1. P ≥ 3: the three copies on three different pages (P = 3: g, g+1,
  g+2; P = 7: g, g+2, g+4; P = 13: g, g+4, g+8). The page and the slot of
  every copy follow from its index and N; nothing is random.
- The slot of copy k of index i in its band is a fixed design, the same for
  every group and every page (a shorter group's copies are a subset of a
  full group's):
  - Copy 0 of index i goes to band-0 data slot i in block raster order,
    block (r0, c).
  - Copy 1 goes to band 1, column PI1[c]; copy 2 to band 2, column PI2[c].
    Each sits at the free (non-bullseye) row of the same RANK in its column
    as r0 has in band 0's column c, ranks counted top down.
  - Column sizes: bands 0 and 2 have 9 free rows in columns 2, 5, 8, 11, 14
    and 17; band 1 has 9 in columns 2, 8 and 14; every other column has 10.
    PI1 never maps a 10-row column onto a 9-row one. In band 2 four columns
    change size: columns 12 and 20 (10 rows) map onto 9-row columns and drop
    band-0 rank SKIP[c] (column 12 drops rank 9, column 20 drops rank 0;
    later ranks move up one); the dropped chunk's copy 2 goes to
    OVERFLOW[c]; columns 5 and 14 (9 rows) map onto 10-row columns and leave
    rank 9 (row 29) unreached — exactly the two OVERFLOW blocks.

        PI1      = 11 13 15 16 20 10 12  1 14 17 18 19  0  6  8  9  3  2  4  7  5
        PI2      =  6  7  8  9 15  0  1 16  2  3  4  5 17 18 19 20 10 11 12 13 14
        SKIP     = {5: 9, 12: 9, 14: 9, 20: 0}
        OVERFLOW = {12: (29, 19), 20: (29, 0)}

- Fourth copies. The slots of page q left empty, pooled in page raster
  order, carry fourth copies of group f(q) = (q + s) mod P, s the smallest
  s ≥ 1 with s ≢ −d and s ≢ −2d (mod P): a group with no copy on page q,
  so its fourth copy lies on a fourth page (such an s exists for P ≥ 4; it
  is 1, except 2 at P = 5). For P ≤ 3 f(q) = q, the group whose copy 0 is
  on page q. Its chunks in index order, once each, as many as fit; the
  rest are white. A full group leaves 3 empty slots per page (band 1's
  unreached ones), a short last group the rest of its bands on its three
  pages. A payload may be as short as one chunk; an empty one is refused.
- Guarantees of the slot geometry (MEASURED exactly on the design, every
  group size): one copy per band; block columns pairwise at least 5 apart;
  copies 0 and 2 at least 19 block rows apart; the narrowest straight
  strip, at any angle, that touches the interiors of all three copies of a
  chunk is at least 91 modules = 46.2 mm (minimum 46.30 mm; median 72 mm);
  the smallest disc touching all three has radius at least 79.9 mm; no
  line through two copies passes within 7.36 columns (140/19) of the
  third. Copy k of index i sits at the same slot on whichever page carries
  it, so the overlay of all the pages is the single-page design and these
  numbers hold for damage at the same place on every sheet (a fold, a
  stain or a hole through the stack; every placed copy checked at its
  design slot for every P up to 13, and the overlay measured directly at
  P = 3 and 13: the same minima). So a straight fold or
  scratch at any angle, or a straight strip under 46.3 mm, takes at most
  two copies of a chunk (a line through two copies takes two: the
  guarantee is that one survives); a disc under 159.8 mm across at most
  two, under 77.9 mm at most one; a vertical band under 35.1 mm or a
  horizontal band under 69.6 mm at most one; a torn corner needs legs over
  about 210 × 211 mm, an edge strip over 104.9 mm deep, and no quarter
  frame holds all three. Damage on one sheet only, for P ≥ 3, takes at
  most one placed copy of any chunk, whatever its shape (at P = 3 a
  page's fourth copies are of the group whose copy 0 it carries, so a
  sheet may hold two copies of those chunks; for P ≥ 4 a fourth copy is
  on a fourth page). NOT covered: a bent tear
  through the stack (194.5 mm suffice to take index 188); the left or
  right half of EVERY page lost along the vertical centre line (9 or 10
  indices of every group lose all three copies — the design's indices
  whose copies sit in columns 5/10/0 or 15/9/20; a design that puts a copy
  on each side of the centre line reaches only 8.74 mm of straight strip,
  the owner's ruling); two independent damages; three lost pages of the
  form {g, g+d, g+2d} mod P (below). The guarantees are measured on the
  16 × 16 interiors; counting each block's timing frame the strip minimum
  is 44.9 mm.
- Page loss (MEASURED exactly: every subset of lost pages, 2^P of them,
  for P = 1 .. 13 at the fullest and the shortest payload of P pages —
  the fullest at P = 13 being 2,521 chunks, 65,535 B — and at 557
  chunks; r2/placement_stats.py --page-loss). A
  subset is fatal when some chunk has all three placed copies on lost
  pages. For P ≥ 2 any one page may be lost, for P ≥ 3 any two; for
  P ≥ 3 the fatal three-page subsets are exactly {g, g+d, g+2d} mod P,
  every fatal subset contains one, and one sheet carries at most one
  placed copy of a chunk.

        4   1   g g+1 g+2              0 / 0 / 4  (every 3-set)           yes
        5   2   g g+2 g+4              0 / 0 / 5                          yes ({0, 1, 3} = {g, g+2, g+4} at g = 1)
        6   2   g g+2 g+4              0 / 0 / 2                          no
        7   2   g g+2 g+4              0 / 0 / 7                          no
        8   3   g g+3 g+6              0 / 0 / 8                          no
        9   3   g g+3 g+6              0 / 0 / 3                          no

  Counting the fourth copies as well, no new fatal subset appears; with
  full groups the table is the same (3 fourth copies a page), with a short

  in the last group: only all four pages are fatal).
- History: the first v1 draft (2026-10-20, morning) put copy k in column
  third (i + k) mod 3 — one third of the chunks then had all three copies on
  the page's main diagonal and a diagonal scratch took one chunk whole in
  simulation; the second (afternoon) was a seeded shuffle repaired to a
  straight-line constraint, minimum strip 6 mm; the design above is the
  optimum of its family (two band-2 crossings; 46.30 mm; any higher bound is
  infeasible), found by bootstrap/r2/design_placement.py. Until the
  amendment of 2026-10-20 night page p carried chunks 204p .. 204p + 203,
  all three copies of each on that page (a lost page lost its 204 chunks);
  now the copies of a chunk go to different pages at the same slots. A
  reader never needs the placement: every block says which chunk it is.
- Capacity: 204 chunks = 5,304 bytes per page; a 65,535-byte payload (the
  2-byte length) is 13 pages.
- Multi-page guarantees: pages in any order; duplicate frames and pages are
  harmless; N follows from the length, and the pages that carry a missing
  chunk j from j and N (copy k of group j // 204 on page
  (j // 204 + k·d) mod P) — a reader never needs it, it pools by index;
  a wrong result needs a CRC-32 collision on the assembled data. A
  destroyed page costs each chunk at most one placed copy for P ≥ 3: any
  one page may be lost (P ≥ 2), any two (P ≥ 3), three only if they are
  not {g, g+d, g+2d} mod P (table above); for P = 2 or 3 each page alone
  holds a copy of every chunk. Not guaranteed: payload identity is 48 bits
  (length + CRC-32), two payloads of equal length can share it only by a
  CRC collision; a block's CRC is bound to its payload's header, so blocks
  of another payload never validate.

## CRC-32
- The one check function of the page (blocks, header, data), the one the
  Plate explains: r = ffffffff; for each byte b: r = r XOR b, then eight
  times: r = (r >> 1) XOR edb88320 if r is odd, else r >> 1; the CRC is
  r XOR ffffffff. Stored high byte first. The header's 20-bit check is the
  CRC modulo 2^20. (The typed loader's line check is a different, shorter
  function, the Horner of TYPED-STREAM.md: the loader is typed verbatim and
  never re-implemented, so it needs no explanation; ruled 2026-10-20.)

## Decoding (what a reader does; the steps are the TYPED reader's)
What the format guarantees a reader: every bullseye block carries the header;
every data block carries its global chunk index under a CRC-32 bound to that
header; the timing lattice is a local clock and a local black/white
reference; a frame need not show the whole page, nor be upright, nor read
every block — the payload is meant to close from a SET of frames, pooled,
and the finder photographs again where a frame falls short (ruling 15 of
2026-10-20). The steps below are what the typed reader b.py does
(r2reader/reader_lite_v1.py as amended 2026-10-22; the print of record's
reader differs as listed under "Changes of 2026-10-22"); the readable
reference r2reader/reader.py does
more (per-tile thresholds, a lattice snap and least-squares pose, header
Chase, refit walks, Chase repair). The measured record is
r2reader/diet/LADDER-2026-10-20.md and the refuter's panel 21 in
REVIEW-2026-10-20.md.
1. Binarise the frame to find bullseyes, with one threshold midway between
   the darkest and the brightest of a 1-in-97 subsample of its pixels. It
   serves only the search, and it must not follow the paper: the page of a
   short payload is nine-tenths white, and a mean or a low percentile there
   sits next to the paper level and turns the blurred white rings to ink. A
   bullseye a blurred or unevenly lit frame does not show is simply not a
   seed: the others, or the next frame, cover its part. The typed reader's
   floor, as measured on the 10-20 diet reader (ruling 15): a whole page at
   2 samples per module reads at σ 0.45 module of blur; at 1.5 samples per
   module it needs σ ≤ 0.4 (at 1.5 and σ 0.45 it finds 2 of 15 bullseyes and
   reads nothing; the readable reader reads that frame). Closer frames have
   less blur in module units.
2. Find bullseyes: thirteen alternating one-module runs along a row (seven
   ink, six white — the centre dot must survive as a run of its own),
   framed by at least a quarter module of white, the inner runs on the
   grid set by the outer ink runs' centres (12 modules apart) within
   0.3 module, confirmed by the same comb along the column through the
   middle (within one module of the row) and by the ring pattern (75 % of
   the ring modules). One pitch per bullseye, the mean of the row and
   column combs; a candidate within 6 modules of a found bullseye is
   skipped; the whole block must lie in the frame.
3. Pose: ONE rotation and quarter turn for the whole frame — those at which
   the 68 timing modules around all its bullseye blocks (rows −9 and +8
   from the centre, 18 modules each; columns −9 and +8, 16 each) agree best
   with the alternating pattern, searched over the four turns and rotations
   in steps of about 1° to ±9° (so a frame tilted beyond about 10° reads
   nothing: hold the camera square to the page); a tie in the score goes
   to the smallest rotation. The timing frame lies
   9 modules out on the left and top of a centre and 8 on the right and
   bottom, so the timing frames alone tell the turn (margin 1.65–2.4× over
   the wrong turns at 2 % perspective; it inverts at 4 %, outside the
   targets). Each bullseye becomes a seed: its comb centre, unrefined, and
   the comb's pitch along both axes turned by that rotation; its timing
   phase from the sign of its correlation. Measured against the truth over
   520 seeds in 59 frames: worst axis angle 2.7°, worst length 5 %. No
   lattice-vector snap, no least squares, no homography (the 10-20 ladder:
   following a single clock line out from a bullseye fails at σ 0.3–0.45,
   since the one-module alternation keeps a third of the contrast of the
   data rows beside it).
4. Header: until a header is known, read the 68 header bits around each
   seed, at the seed as placed (the comb centre with the frame's rotation
   and turn, not refined), in a plain read with no flips, until one passes
   its 20-bit check; it gives the length and the CRC-32 of the data. The
   header is needed before any block is accepted: every block's CRC-32
   covers it. The FIRST header to pass is the payload's for the rest of the
   session (the header is the same on every page of a payload); later
   frames are checked against it without reading their own, so a frame of
   another payload accepts nothing (measured: 0 blocks). A frame without a
   readable header reads if an earlier frame gave the header; while no
   header is known a frame is not walked and reads nothing (shoot it again,
   or another frame first; on the measured near-white page (the stand-in's
   last page before the amendment of 2026-10-20 night, 45 chunks — a
   1-page payload of 45 chunks lays out the same), three of six partial
   frames give no header alone — 3 or 4 of the 68 bits wrong at the
   unrefined seed — and read once another frame has given it). A false
   first header, about 1 in 2^20 per failing read, stalls the session until
   it is started again.
5. Walk: breadth first from every seed at once, every seed walked. A
   block is placed at a placed neighbour's centre plus that neighbour's
   17-module step, refined against its 68 timing modules (a 3×3 search at
   ½, ¼, ⅛ and 1/16 module; a score tie goes to the candidate nearest the
   current estimate, never to the largest offset), and its own two steps
   are re-measured to those of its neighbours nearer the seed that the
   walk passed through, so the walk follows perspective and shear (up to
   3.7° between the axes at 2 % perspective). The walk passes through a
   bullseye, a block whose CRC checks and a white slot; a block whose CRC
   fails is a wall it goes round. A block whose centre lies within 10
   modules of the frame's edge is not read and ends the walk there; a
   block whose predicted centre falls in a 5-module cell of the frame
   already reached is skipped (which also bounds the walk: the cells are
   finite, the reader cannot hang).
6. Read: each module is sampled at its centre and sharpened by half its
   4-neighbour Laplacian; the block's threshold lies midway between the 9th
   darkest and the 9th brightest of its 68 timing modules; a block with
   three or fewer ink modules is skipped as white. The reader pads the
   frame with one image's worth of mid-grey (128) after its last row, so
   no sample leaves the frame's buffer: a sample left or right of the
   frame reads the adjacent row, one below or above it (wrapping) reads
   the grey, and the grey moves neither the darkest nor the brightest of
   step 1's subsample, so the threshold is unchanged on any frame that
   holds both ink and paper (ruling 59, 2026-10-23; until then every
   sample was clamped to the frame). Accept a data block iff the CRC-32 over the payload's six header bytes
   followed by the block's bytes 0–27 equals its bytes 28–31: the plain read
   first, then each single-module flip over all 256 modules (the block
   code's minimum distance is 6, so a read with four or fewer wrong
   modules can never be mis-accepted; a false accept needs five or more
   and then 257·2⁻³² per read — 0 in 1.69 million trials measured). Pool
   chunks by index across blocks, frames and pages, the last value per
   index winning (a false accept is healed by any later read of that
   chunk).
7. Done when chunks 0 .. N−1 are present and the data's CRC-32 equals the
   header's; decode the data with the loader's decoder (primer: the
   reader's own bytes, the file b.py), check the result's CRC-32 against
   the baked constant B2, and write the next stage, c.py (the stage names
   of ruling 34). A changed reader file (other line ends, an extra
   newline) or pages compressed for another reader end in AssertionError
   and no file; pages of
   another payload add nothing (step 4).
- Input: file names one per line on standard input at the prompt
  'file: ', an empty line stops. A PGM, P5 (the text P5, width, height,
  255, one byte per pixel; the reader takes width and height from the
  first bytes and the last width × height bytes as the image), and nothing
  else. The loupe path: the module grid typed as text, H lines of W
  characters 0/1, goes through the Plate's one-liner
  (r2reader/grid_to_pgm.py; since 2026-10-23 it takes no arguments: it
  reads grid.txt and writes grid.pgm in the working directory), which
  writes it as a P5 PGM at 1 pixel per
  module, 0 for ink ('1'), 255 for white, with a 6-module white border on
  every side (short lines padded white; a character sorting below '1' is
  white, any other ink); the reader reads that file as any other capture.
  A file that cannot be read prints 'cannot read' and is skipped. One
  progress line per frame, after the prompt line that names it: the
  bullseyes found, '@' as the target mark, then the chunks held / the
  header's chunk count ('?' before a header is read), e.g. '9@ 500/557';
  no name, no blocks count, no done flag, the exit with c.py written being
  the done signal.
  Several inputs, in any order, pages mixed.

## What the camera simulator must reproduce (qualification only)
- Whole pages at 1.5 and 2.0 samples per module; partial frames (quarters and
  thirds of the page) at 2 to 4 samples per module; Gaussian blur σ up to 0.45
  module; perspective with corners displaced up to 2 % of the frame diagonal;
  rotation 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° and ±4°; illumination gradient ±20 %; additive
  noise σ = 10/255; a 300-dpi flatbed case without perspective. Damage:
  blacked or torn bullseyes, blots, scratches, a missing corner.
  These are the targets a reader is measured against, not promises printed on
  the page: the page says to photograph closer and more than once. The typed
  reader's measured floor (10-20): a whole page needs 2 samples per module at
  σ 0.45, or 1.5 at σ 0.4; tilt within 10°; perspective within 2 %.
