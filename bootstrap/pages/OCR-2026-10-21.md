# OCR confusion test of the data leaf — 2026-10-21

The question: which font, and which glyph set, lets a phone OCR read the
R1 data leaf with the fewest retyped lines? Three things were asked:
change the font (OCR-B is the candidate), consider uppercase, and check
whether base 36 (0-9 A-Z, read by `int(t, 36)`, about 95 fewer typed
glyphs) holds up. This is a simulation only. Nothing was printed or
photographed. Instrument: `bootstrap/pages/ocr_confusion.py`. Derived
outputs (images, OCR text, `results.json`, `params.json`, the full tables
`tables.md` / `compact.md`) are under `bootstrap/build/ocr/`, which is
gitignored.

**The caveat that governs everything below.** The OCR here is tesseract
5.5.3, not the phone camera's OCR. On these leaves tesseract is far worse than
the phone camera was. On a perfect, undegraded render of today's leaf (Menlo,
lowercase), tesseract got 48 of 48 lines wrong (MEASURED). The phone camera got
17 of 48 wrong on the real paper. Its errors also differ in kind. It
reads Menlo's slashed zero as `@` (194 times over the test grid), `1` as
`l` or `i`, and `7` as `/`. The phone camera never read `@` or `/`; it read 1 as
l once (line 21) and never as i. So the
absolute line error rates below say nothing about the phone. What
carries over: the ranking between fonts and glyph sets under one fixed
reader, and which glyph pairs collide. Phone-scale line rates are given
only as ESTIMATES (see "Calibration").

## Method

- **Leaves.** For each of 6 fonts × 4 glyph sets: one synthetic data
  leaf of 48 lines, set in the real data-page layout of `render_pdf.py`:
  - 2.35 mm cells, the face sized so `M` fills one cell (as
    `font_for_width`);
  - the line number right-aligned in two cells, then one cell;
  - groups of 5 with half a cell between them;
  - the 4-glyph check in the fixed check column;
  - line pitch 4.318 mm below a 10 mm margin.

  Lines 1–47 carry 60 glyphs and line 48 carries 56 (2,876 glyphs), plus
  48 × 4 check glyphs. Glyphs come from a seeded shuffle of the glyph set
  repeated, so every glyph appears equally often. That is 96 per glyph
  per leaf for base 32 and 85 for base 36. Seed 20261021 + the set size;
  each set's content is the same for every font.

  One layout difference from the real leaf: the real leaf 4 has 21 prose
  lines above the data, so its data lines 43–48 spill onto a second
  sheet. The synthetic leaf has the 48 data lines on one sheet and no
  prose.
- **Fonts.** These sizes are the 300-dpi point sizes that fill a cell:
  - Menlo (`/System/Library/Fonts/Menlo.ttc`, 44 pt);
  - Courier New (45);
  - Andale Mono (45);
  - PT Mono (`PTMono.ttc`, 45);
  - SF Mono (`SFNSMono.ttf`, 43);
  - OCR-B (`OCR-B-Regular.otf` from `scratch/fonts/ocrb-2015-10-27.zip`,
    unzipped to `bootstrap/build/ocr/fonts/`, 41).
- **Glyph sets.**
  - A32L: today's `0123456789abcdefghjkmnpqrstvwxyz`.
  - A32U: the same set in uppercase.
  - B36U: `0-9A-Z`.
  - B36L: `0-9a-z`.
- **Capture model.** Each leaf is rendered at twice the shot's resolution.
  Then:
  - rotation of 1–2° (random sign), as a homography whose corners are
    each displaced within a disc of 1–2 % of the diagonal (the
    corner-in-a-disc model of `bootstrap/r2/camera.py`, whose
    `homography` and `gauss_blur` are imported);
  - 2× area downsample;
  - Gaussian blur σ ∈ {0.6, 1.0, 1.4} output px;
  - camera.py's "nominal" photometry: paper 0.80, ink 0.10, background
    0.30, illumination gradient ±20 % along a random direction, additive
    noise σ 10/255;
  - JPEG quality 85.

  Two scales:
  - WHOLE: the whole page at 14.63 px/mm (3,072 px across 210 mm, the phone camera's frame width).
  - HALF: two shots at 29 px/mm, lines 1–24 and 25–48, each with 6 mm
    of paper around the lines.

  Seeds are 7100 + 1000·scale + 100·blur + part. They are the same for
  every font and glyph set, so the comparison is paired. Every shot's
  drawn rotation, perspective and gradient direction sit in its `.json`
  beside the image.
- **Extra conditions.**
  - CLEAN: the undegraded render at the whole-page scale, lossless.
    This is tesseract's own floor.
  - NOISE 3: the whole-page shot repeated with noise σ 3/255, an
    HDR-like denoised capture, at all three blurs.
- **OCR.** `tesseract IMG stdout --oem 1 --psm 6 -l eng --dpi
  <px/mm × 25.4>`, LSTM engine, one block of text. The primary condition
  has no whitelist. The secondary adds `-c
  tessedit_char_whitelist=<the glyph set>`.
- **Scoring.**
  - OCR lines are matched to truth lines by a monotone alignment on
    difflib similarity.
  - Each matched line is then aligned with Levenshtein distance against
    "line number + 64 typed glyphs", spaces removed. Errors on the line
    number, and insertions before the first data glyph, are not counted
    (the number is not typed).
  - A line with any remaining error = one retry.
  - Three ways to accept a read:
    - STRICT: exact, as `a.py` today ("a capital is a typo").
    - FOLDED: case folded to the set's case.
    - MAPPED: folded, and for base 32 also the four look-alikes the
      alphabet leaves out mapped to the glyph the leaf names (i, l → 1;
      o → 0; u → v). This is what a loader with one `str.translate` would
      accept. Base 36 has no free look-alikes, so its MAPPED equals
      FOLDED.
    - MAPPED+@: MAPPED, and `@` → 0 as well, in every set. `@` is valid
      in no set, so this map is as free as O → 0. It was added on the
      refuter's correction M2.
  - Scoring detail: an insertion between the line number and the first
    data glyph is counted as an error. Over all 624 shots that is 147–150
    insertions in 1,472,640 scored glyphs, and 5–8 of 23,040 lines are
    wrong only because of it (MEASURED). It is left in, so the published
    numbers did not move.
- **Subsets.**
  - GRID: the noise-10 grid, whole + half × 3 blurs (9 page-equivalents
    of 48 lines per cell).
  - STABLE: CLEAN plus every blur-1.4 shot (whole noise 10, both halves,
    whole noise 3), 4 page-equivalents, 192 lines and 12,272 glyphs per
    cell. Why the subset exists: tesseract does not degrade monotonically
    with blur. At σ 0.6–1.0 its segmentation often fails, dropping groups
    or whole lines. One cell reads 58 % glyph error (OCR-B A32U, noise 3,
    σ 1.0). σ 1.4, which smooths the noise, is best in every row. A phone
    pipeline denoises, which argues for STABLE. But STABLE is a subset I
    chose after seeing the data, and some rankings change between the two
    subsets. Both are shown, and the answers rely only on what holds in
    both.
  - No replicate seeds were run. Every cell is one draw of content and
    one draw of capture geometry, so there is no measured spread to
    judge small differences against.
- **Run cost.** 624 OCR calls (432 grid + 48 clean + 144 noise-3), about
  6.5 minutes on 6 workers.

## Calibration against the paper session (question d)

- **Paper session.** Menlo lowercase on the phone camera: 17 of 48 lines
  retyped = 35.4 % line error. Under independent glyphs that is about
  0.68 % per glyph (ESTIMATE).
- **Tesseract, same font and set, STRICT** (all MEASURED):

  | condition | glyph error | line error |
  |---|---|---|
  | GRID | 8.19 % | 97.9 % |
  | STABLE | 4.35 % | 96 % |
  | CLEAN | 4.11 % | 100 % |
  | NOISE 3, σ 1.4 | 3.5 % | — |

- **The verdict.** The synthetic condition is far from 35 %. The only
  calibration by degradation tried was cutting the noise from 10 to 3;
  it changed nothing systematic. Blur, scale and JPEG were not varied
  further, because the undegraded page already fails every line.
- **The calibration actually used.** Tesseract's per-glyph error is
  scaled so that Menlo lowercase STRICT lands on the phone camera's 35.4 %:
  - k = 0.157 on STABLE (0.083 on GRID; 0.268 / 0.120 with the
    whitelist);
  - then line error = 1 − (1 − k·ε)^64.

  These are the "ESTIMATE (phone-scaled)" columns. They assume the phone camera
  is uniformly k times better than tesseract on every glyph, which is
  untested. Read them for ranking, not as forecasts.
- **The retry model.** The ESTIMATES count p/(1 − p) extra attempts per
  line. At the phone camera's p = 17/48 that is 26.3 extra attempts for 48
  lines, against the 23 measured on paper. The model overstates retries
  by about 14 %.
- **Direction check on the phone camera's own pairs (Menlo lowercase GRID,
  MEASURED).** PAPER-2026-10-20's confusion table is written "as first
  typed → as accepted", that is, what was read → what was true. In
  truth → read terms:
  - The phone camera read truth q as g 7 times. Tesseract reads q → g 46 times:
    the same direction.
  - The phone camera read truth y as v 4 times. Tesseract reads y → v 0 times
    and v → y 6 times: the opposite direction.
  - The phone camera read truth 1 as l once. Tesseract reads 1 → l 73 times:
    the same direction.
  - The phone camera read the slashed zero as `Ø` once. Tesseract reads it as
    `@` 194 times.

## Answers

These are the answers as corrected after the refuter's review.

**(a) Which font is best for OCR of the data leaf?**

This test does not pick one font among Andale Mono, OCR-B and Menlo.

- **What holds in both subsets.** In uppercase with the look-alike map,
  Andale Mono and OCR-B are the best two (STABLE 1.83 / 2.38 %, GRID
  4.20 / 4.76 %, MEASURED). Courier New is worst or near-worst on GRID.
- **Why Menlo cannot be ruled out.** Much of Menlo's penalty is
  tesseract reading its slashed zero as `@`. `@` is valid in no glyph
  set, so a loader can map it to 0 for free, just as it maps O to 0.
  With that map, STABLE uppercase reads Andale 1.72 %, Menlo 2.10 %,
  OCR-B 2.38 %, and lowercase reads Menlo 2.06 %, Andale 2.43 %, OCR-B
  3.44 % (MEASURED).
- **The whitelist does not settle it.** With the whitelist, OCR-B
  uppercase is lowest (1.90 %), but Andale (2.15 %) and Menlo (2.57 %)
  are close.
- **Order flips between subsets.** The lowercase order changes between
  GRID and STABLE, and no replicate seeds were run.
- **Withdrawn.** The earlier "OCR-B has a modest edge over Menlo" is
  withdrawn.
- **Where OCR-B stands.** OCR-B is no worse than any face tested, but it
  is not shown to be better than Andale Mono or Menlo.

**(b) Does uppercase help?**

Partly, and it depends on the font and the subset.

- **It removes g/q under tesseract.** Lowercase q → g 20–70 per font,
  against uppercase Q → G 0–4 (GRID, MEASURED). The phone camera's 7 q-read-as-g
  slips are in the same direction as tesseract's, so this is the pair
  uppercase most plausibly cures on the phone too.
- **It does not remove v/y under tesseract.** Per font, v↔y against V↔Y
  is Menlo 6 vs 5, Andale 4 vs 4, PT Mono 1 vs 1, OCR-B 9 vs 1, Courier
  New 16 vs 7, SF Mono 7 vs 2. The phone camera's 4 slips were truth y read as
  v, a direction tesseract almost never produces. Whether uppercase
  cures them is unmeasured.
- **Fonts where it helps in both subsets** (MAPPED, lowercase →
  uppercase): Andale (GRID 6.77 → 4.20, STABLE 2.49 → 1.83), OCR-B
  (7.49 → 4.76, 3.44 → 2.38). Courier New improves on STABLE only.
- **Fonts where the verdict flips between subsets:** Menlo (GRID worse,
  STABLE flat) and PT Mono (GRID better, STABLE worse).
- **New uppercase risks:** 5 → S, 9 → O, Z → 2, G → 6, and J → I/1 in PT
  Mono, SF Mono and Menlo.
- **The look-alike map matters.** Uppercase pays most when the loader
  maps the absent look-alikes (O → 0, I and L → 1, U → V, and `@` → 0).
  `a.py` does not map them today.

**(c) Is base-36 uppercase healthy under the best font?**

No. Under every reader measured here base 36 does not pay.

- **The structural reason.**
  - Base 32 has exactly one valid glyph per look-alike class: 0 for
    0/O/`@`, 1 for 1/I/L, V for V/U. A context-free map therefore
    repairs every confusion within a class, whatever the reader.
  - Base 36 makes both glyphs of 0/O and 1/I valid, so no map can repair
    them.
- **The size of the penalty depends on the reader.** Tesseract pulls
  OCR-B's 0 to O 64–67 % of the time (227 of 340 STABLE, 329 of 510
  GRID, whitelist or not, MEASURED).
- **Under each set's free map** (MAPPED), base 36 is worse in every font
  and case, in both subsets (MEASURED). That costs +305 to +2,369 typed
  characters (ESTIMATE) against a saving of 98.
- **Under STRICT scoring** (no map, as `a.py` reads today), the picture
  is mixed:
  - Some rows favour base 36 (STABLE PT Mono upper; GRID Courier upper,
    Andale lower, PT Mono lower).
  - The uppercase balance is PT Mono −199, SF Mono −50, Andale +256,
    OCR-B +207, Menlo +176 characters (ESTIMATE).
  - The fair comparison gives each set its free map, and there base 36
    loses.
- **A check-guided repair.** One way to win back base 36's ambiguity:
  try the O/0 and I/1 alternatives until the line check passes. It would
  cost code in `a.py` and raise the false-accept rate to about 2^7/2^20
  per line (ESTIMATE). It was not measured.

**(d) Menlo lowercase versus the paper session's 17 of 48.**

- **The comparison.** The synthetic tesseract rate is far above it: 98 %
  (GRID), 96 % (STABLE), and 100 % even on the undegraded render, against
  the phone camera's 35 % (MEASURED).
- **Why.** The gap is the reader, not the degradation. Tesseract's
  per-glyph error on a clean render (4.1 %) is about six times the phone camera's estimated 0.68 %.
- **Calibration by degradation.** The only attempt was cutting the noise
  from 10 to 3. It did not move the rate toward 35 %.
- **Rescaling instead.** The rates were rescaled per glyph (k = 0.157 on
  STABLE), so Menlo lowercase STRICT lands on 35 % by construction.
- **What the rescaling gives (ESTIMATE).** Line error in uppercase with
  the free maps (MAPPED+@):
  - Andale about 16 %;
  - Menlo about 19 %;
  - OCR-B about 21 %;
  - Menlo lowercase about 19 %.

  That is roughly 8–10 retyped lines of 48 against the 17 measured.
- **Caveat.** The retry model behind these estimates overstates the
  paper session's own retries by about 14 % (26.3 against 23).

## What did not go as planned

- **Tesseract cannot be calibrated to the phone camera by degradation.** Its
  clean-render floor is above the phone's measured rate. Only a noise
  cut was tried. The calibration became a per-glyph scale factor, an
  ESTIMATE.
- **Segmentation failures dominate the low-blur noise-10 shots.** Groups
  and lines get dropped, making single grid cells erratic (6–58 % glyph
  error for the same font across blur). No replicate seeds were run, so
  small differences have no measured spread. Rankings that flip between
  GRID and STABLE are listed above.
- **The half-page shots read worse than the whole page at the same blur
  in px.** Scale is confounded with relative blur here (a fixed σ in px
  is a smaller blur relative to the glyph at 29 px/mm), so this does not
  say which scale tesseract prefers.
- **The synthetic leaf's layout differs from the real leaf 4.** It has no
  prose and keeps all 48 lines on one sheet.
- **OCR-B was already present** when the run started; nothing is
  pending.
