LENGTH. The bar under this line is 2362 printer dots at 600 per inch
= 99.991 mm. It defines the mm. Use ratios: paper grows and shrinks.

THE COARSE PAGES (the next step). A grid of square modules, each 12 x
12 printer dots = 0.508 mm; ink = 1. Every 17th row and column is a
timing line of ink and paper in turn: the clock and the ink/paper
reference. Between them sit blocks of 16 x 16 modules. Fifteen blocks,
spread over the page, hold a target: rings of ink and paper, one
module wide, thirteen across the middle, ink at both ends. The modules
of such a block outside the rings, read row by row, high bit first,
are the header: 2 bytes length of the data, 4 bytes CRC-32 of the
data, 20 bits CRC-32 of those 6 bytes (its low 20 bits). Every other
block is 32 bytes, read row by row, high bit first: 2 bytes chunk
number n, 26 bytes = bytes 26n to 26n+25 of the data, 4 bytes CRC-32
of the 6 header bytes then the first 28. Each chunk is printed 3 times
at scattered places. Use a block only if its CRC is right; stop when
the data's CRC is right. Photograph the page in pieces, close up: a
piece that shows a target can be read once any piece gave the header.
KEEP THIS BOOK. Dry, dark, cool. No tape, no glue. Copy the whole
book, then check the copy: its 8 x 8 block must still read Hello 42.
The book crosses the centuries as copies, not as one paper. A mirror
image on a page is a ghost from the facing page: read the sharp
original. Brown paper: look with light from the side, light from
behind, or infrared; carbon ink stays black. A page has 15 ring
targets; one in a picture is enough once the header is known.
