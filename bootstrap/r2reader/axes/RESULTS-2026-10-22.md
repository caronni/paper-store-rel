# RESULTS-2026-10-22: fix #2 (each block's own pose) and fix0 (the tie fix), measured against the reader of record

The question behind this note: JUDGE-2026-10-22.md ruled that the up-left
misses are a code bug, from `ft`'s one-sided tie-break. It asked for "fix
#2": fit each block's own pose (translation, A and B) on its own 68-module
frame, with no one-sided tie-break.

Built here:
- **fix2**, which is fix #2 in two variants (plus a seed-refit variant);
- **fix0**, which is the judge's one-line tie fix;
- one **probe**.

Each was measured against `reader_lite_v1.py` on the same captures.
Nothing here edits an existing file, the print of record, the stage-1
build, `reader_lite_v1.py` or `reader.py`.

## Short answer

- **On the grids and the exact raster the up-left pattern is gone in
  every arm, fix0 included.** On the
  stage-1 grids the reader of record misses 15 up-left blocks (595 / 593 /
  591 of 609 chunks). Every arm reads all three grids to 609 and closes.
  The instrument counts 0 unread blocks in every direction class on page
  0's grid (pages 1 and 2 by chunk count). On blurred renders some up-left
  blocks stay unread in every arm (b045 seed 2 p0: 3 / 3 / 4 / 2 / 3 / 1),
  with no diagonal preferred.
- **fix #2 as specified is a regression on blurred 2-samples-per-module
  renders.** On the camera seed the earlier notes used (seed 1) it looks
  better: nominal-b030 p0 / p1 / p2 go from 601 / 604 / 605 to 609 / 609 /
  609. Over a seed sweep it is not:

  | renders | fix2 (i) vs reader of record |
  |---|---|
  | nominal-b030, seeds 2-3 | -81 chunks over 6 pages; worse on 5 of the 6 |
  | nominal-b045, seeds 2-4 | -635 chunks over 9 pages; worse on all 9 |
  | b045 three-page pools, seeds 2 and 3 | the reader of record closes both, fix2 closes neither |

  Variant (ii) does better than (i) but also loses at blur 0.45: -337 over
  9 pages, and the same two pools fail.
- **Why, measured against truth:** at 2 samples per module, the axes
  fitted on the block's own frame are noisier than the learned axes. The
  learned axes are the difference of two centres one block apart.

  | b045 seed-2 p0 | median axis error | median centre error |
  |---|---|---|
  | reader of record | 1.01 % | 0.085 module |
  | fix2 (i) | 1.46 % | 0.118 module |

  fix2 does cut the worst-case tail: the 90th-percentile axis error drops
  from 3.10 % to 1.59 % on b030 p0, and from 2.95 % to 0.91 % at spm 7. At
  spm 7 the fit beats the learned axes on the p90 axis error, the centre
  and the far-corner error; the median axis error ties (0.52 vs 0.54 %).
- **On the photos**, all arms hold the same chunks, and every arm closes
  both pools (1+2+3 and 1+2+4) to the photos' bundle. fix2 lowers the
  unread data blocks summed over the four photos from 16 to 7 (1/3/9/3 to
  1/1/5/0). It costs about 2.2-2.5× the walk wall.
- **fix0 is the fix the measurements support for the up-left bug**: +21
  typed characters, no systematic cost elsewhere: exact raster 60 → 608,
  grids → 609, the nominal renders and photos within ±2 chunks, wall
  unchanged. Away from exact ties its effect is per-render noise, not
  zero: on the refuter's held-out envelope-edge renders (spm 1.5, blur
  0.40, seeds 1-4) single pages move by -15 to +9 chunks, net +18 over
  10 pages, the pools -1 / +1 / 0; perfect spm 2 594 vs 596.
- **The probe** (variant (ii) with only the two finer axis steps) is the
  only fix #2 form that beat the reader of record on the seed sweep. It is
  one configuration found after looking at these same renders. On the
  refuter's held-out envelope-edge renders it gains per page (+42 over 10
  pages) but loses every three-page pool (599 / 599 / 597 against 601 /
  602 / 598), so it is a lead and not a result.

## What was built (exact code deltas, typed region only)

Shared by fix2 / fix2b / fix2s / probe: `ft` is replaced by a generic
3×3 search over one 2-vector of the pose: the translation (i = 0), A
(i = 2) or B (i = 3). The search uses the frame correlation `w(s)·(1−2·flip)`
(the sum `ft` maximised, through the reader's own `w`). Ties go to the
candidate nearest the current estimate (`-abs(r)-abs(q)`). Each stage is
coded `c` = 10·i + k, with step = p>>k.

```
def mv(s,i,r,q):
 return s[:i]+[[s[i][0]+r,s[i][1]+q]]+s[i+1:]if i else[s[0]+r,s[1]+q
  ]+s[2:]
```

**fix2.py**, variant (i): fitted axes replace the learned ones and are
handed to the children; the seeds keep fm's pose.

- The reader of record's `ft` (7 lines) becomes `mv` plus:
  ```
  def ft(s,p):
   for c in 5,6,7,8,26,36,27,37,28,38,8:
    i,y=c//10,p>>c%10
    k,k,m,n=max((w(mv(s,i,r,q))*(1-2*s[4]),-abs(r)-abs(q),r,q)
     for r in(-y,0,y)for q in(-y,0,y))
    s[:]=mv(s,i,m,n)
  ```
- In `wk`: `w,f,i,c={},{},[],5*p>>4` becomes `w,i,c={},[],5*p>>4`.
- The 8 lines of translation plus learned axes:
  ```
     m,n=ft(s,p)
     s[0],s[1]=s[0]+m,s[1]+n
     for k,z in((2,b),(3,a)):
      ...
      if g and u:s[k]=[u*(s[0]-g[0]),u*(s[1]-g[1])]
  ```
  become `   ft(s,p)`.
- The read moves into the same `if a or b:` block, ahead of the soundness
  samples. `rb` does not change `s`, so this is the same computation.
- `   f[q,a,b]=s` is deleted.

**fix2b.py**, variant (ii): the learned axes are the start and the fit
goes on top. `ft` takes the stage tuple as `ft(s,p,e)`. In `wk`:

```
   ft(s,p,(5,6,7,8))
   for k,z in((2,b),(3,a)):            # the reader of record's 4 lines, kept
    ...
   ft(s,p,(26,36,27,37,28,38,8))
   x=rb(s)
```

**fix2s.py** is fix2 with `ft(s,p)` moved out of `if a or b:`, so the
seed blocks are fitted too. This is the judge's item 3.

**probe_fix2b_fine.py** is fix2b with the axis stages `(27,37,28,38,8)`:
the two finer steps only.

**fix0.py** is the judge's fix, lines 89-90 of the reader of record:

```
  k,k,m,n=max((sum(v(x+r,y+q)*t for(x,y),t in c),-abs(r-m)-abs(q-n)
   ,r,q)for r in(m-y,m,m+y)for q in(n-y,n,n+y))
```

**The steps.** The reader's `p` is 16 × the module pitch in 1/16 px
(`ft`'s p//32 is half a module). An axis-component step of p>>6, p>>7 and
p>>8 is 1.47 %, 0.74 % and 0.37 % of the 17-module axis. At the ring
(9 modules) that is 0.13, 0.066 and 0.033 module, which is the task's
"about 1/8, 1/16, 1/32 module at the block edge".

The task's parenthetical said "p/32, p/64, p/128". Read in the reader's
`p`, that would be 1/4, 1/8 and 1/16 module at the ring. That wider
schedule (p>>5 to p>>8) was probed as `v_wide` on five seed-1 captures.

## How it was measured

**`measure.py run`**: each reader runs as a plain script (`python -u`) in a
fresh temp dir. The frames go to stdin one line at a time, each written
after the previous progress line, then an empty line.

Two primer modes:

- **S1 (stage-1 pages).** The script is the stage-1 loader's definitions
  (`build/stage1/lower/a.py` stripped, bytes 39..599, as a.py writes them
  into b.py), then the reader's typed text with N2 = 55550 and
  B2 = 2700258880. The primer is the stage-1 `b.py`, copied in as `r.py`.
  For `reader_lite_v1.py` this script is byte for byte the stage-1 build's
  `b.py`, with `'b.py'`/`'c.py'` read as `'r.py'`/`'b.py'`; this is
  checked on every run (`check_runnable`, True). A b.py is counted as
  closed if it equals `build/bundle/b.py` (sha c8ee5267…).

  Why not the reader file itself: its shared `d` uses the 9-order tuple.
  The stage-1 payload is cm2 with a.py's `d` order tuple (0,1,2,3,4,6,8).
  The first attempt with the plain file failed at the B2 assert; it was
  discarded and the run redone.
- **REC (photos).** The script is the reader file with N2 = 50533 and
  B2 = 327353449. The primer is `scratch/typed/r.py` (copied). A b.py is
  counted as closed if it equals `scratch/typed/b.py`, sha 579e5f1c…

**`measure.py sets`**: the owner's acceptance sets. It uses
`diet/w4_sets.sets()` and `diet/w4_harness.run_cases`, with the work dir
moved to `build/axes/measure/w4work` (removed afterwards); `W4_JOBS=8`.

**`instrument_arms.py`** is instrument.py's unread-by-direction method for
readers whose `wk` differs. It execs the arm's own definitions and wraps
only `rb`, which every arm calls with the final pose of each walked
non-seed block. Classes, truth, summaries and the per-block
`analyse`/`summarize` all come from instrument.py, imported unchanged.

Validated on the s1-p0 grid against instrument.py's log for the reader of
record. Both give:
- 632 accepted, 595 chunks;
- UL 15, other 1 unread;
- far corner 0.35 / 0.58;
- axis errors 1.15 / 1.29 %.

The arms' accepted counts equal their plain-run progress lines on every
capture that was compared: photos 1-4, grid, b045 p1. Photos have no
genie fit here: block identity and misread counts only.

**`run_arms.py`** drives `instrument_arms.py`. **`pose_errors.py`** gives
the truth pose errors in the table further down.

**Captures.** Stage-1 lower build, current. Perfect renders = `build/axes/
renders/` (made by the DIAG run). The exact raster = `page_p0.exact600.pgm`
there; checked equal to the .pbm converted (ink 0 / paper 255). New in
`build/axes/measure/frames/`, all `camera.py --preset nominal --seed S` from
the stage-1 pages:
- nominal-b045 (seed 1), pages 0-2;
- a seed sweep: b045 seeds 2, 3, 4 and b030 seeds 2, 3, pages 0-2.

Outputs (`build/axes/measure/`):

- sets-main.json, sizes.json;
- instr/*.json;
- logs/.

## Reading

1. **Does the fix remove the up-left pattern?** Yes, in every arm. It is
   gone as soon as `ft`'s tie stops being one-sided, which confirms the
   judge's ruling. fix0 alone does it on the grids and the exact raster
   for +21 characters and no wall cost. fix #2 does it too, with or
   without refitting the seed.
2. **Does fitting each block's own pose buy more?** Not at 2 samples per
   module, which is the density of the nominal renders and of most
   acceptance-set frames. The fit adds four degrees of freedom on 68 ring
   samples, and on blurred, sample-starved frames it is noisier than the
   17-module centre difference it replaces.

   It cuts the large learned-axis errors the judge targeted on the seed-1
   renders and at spm 7: the p90 axis error falls 3.10 → 1.59 % on b030
   p0 and 2.95 → 0.91 % at spm 7. That is consistent with it closing the
   seed-1 pages. On the sweep renders it does not hold: p90 2.03 → 2.63 %
   on b030 seed 3 (b045 seed 2 still improves, 2.73 → 2.39 %). The median and the
   centre error get worse on every 2-spm render measured, and over the
   seed sweep that costs blocks: 8.0-15.1 % of the 609 chunks per page at
   blur 0.45 (10.2-16.5 % of the reader of record's own count). It also
   fails two b045 pools
   the reader of record closes. Variant (ii) is less bad than (i). Seed
   refitting (fix2s) is no better than (i).

   At 7 samples per module (perfect and nominal spm 7) the fit beats the
   learned axes on the p90 axis error, the centre and the far-corner
   error (the median axis error ties). The photos sit at 7.1-11.1 px per
   module (the DIAG genie fit), but their pose errors were not measured
   here.
3. **What it buys on the photos.** Nothing for closing: the reader of
   record already holds every chunk each photo carries and closes both
   pools. It reads 9 more of the photographed data blocks in total
   (unread 16 → 7): photo 2 3 → 1, photo 3 9 → 5, photo 4 3 → 0. The
   photo-3 blocks still unread are 5; the judge attributes photo 3's
   column-0 losses to translation, not axes, which was not re-checked
   here. The cost is about 2× the walk time.
4. **Recommendation (the owner's call).** Type fix0 if the up-left bug is
   to be fixed. Do not type fix #2 as specified: the refuter's held-out
   envelope-edge renders (spm 1.5, blur 0.40, seeds 1-4) give fix2 -183
   chunks over 10 pages, worse on all 10, pools 588 / 600 / 590 against
   601 / 602 / 598 (fix2b -173). If the per-block fit is
   wanted for the photos' sake, the probe form (learned start plus a ±1 %
   fine fit) is the candidate. It needs held-out renders (other seeds,
   spm 3, the hard preset, quarters) before anything more is claimed for
   it.

## The refuter's and the checker's findings (2026-10-22), and what was done

An Opus refuter reproduced the load-bearing claims (the diff confined to
the walk and `ft`; every progress line it re-ran equal to main.json; the
typed-cost table to the byte; the pooled photos closing to 579e5f1c with

agreeing with the recommendation above), fix0's "within ±2 chunks" holds

gains pages but loses every pool. A Sonnet checker found one wrong range
(the b045 loss), two overstatements (spm 7 "every truth measure"; set I's
partial frames), one unreproduced table (the 54-block comparison), the
header comment of fix2.py over 72 columns, and the direction convention
stated only in DIAG. All corrected above or marked; the header rewrapped.

column offset) from the seed, which equals the page relation in every
table here; the DIAG note's "by image direction" column is the step's
direction in the image. Pointers: `diet/w4_harness.run` and
`diet/w4_sets.sets` are functions, not files; the cm2/b32 sizing uses the
