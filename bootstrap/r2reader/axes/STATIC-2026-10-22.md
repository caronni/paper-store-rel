# The up-left-of-a-bullseye misses: static read of the typed reader (2026-10-22)

Subject: `bootstrap/r2reader/reader_lite_v1.py` (the reader of record),
functions wk, ft, sp, v, f, a, rb, be, ax, fm, compared with
`reader.py`'s walk(). The question: the owner asked whether the misses are
an algorithmic bug, since a physical cause would hit the up-left (a=-1,b=-1),
up-right (-1,+1), down-left (+1,-1) and down-right (+1,+1) neighbours of a
bullseye alike.

The answer is yes, it is a code effect, and it lives in the perfect grid the
2026-10-21 diagnosis was run on. Two `max()` searches break exact score ties
toward their largest candidate.

1. The rotation search in fm. On the 0/1 grid (3 px per module, values 0 and
   255, no blur) the score sum |w| is exactly 130050 at five rotation steps,
   r = -128, -64, 0, 64 and 128. `max((score, r, t))` takes r = +128, a turn
   of 1.79 degrees. The seed axes become A = (815, 25) and B = (-26, 815)
   where the truth is (816, 0) and (0, 816).
2. The translation search in ft. Inside +/-1 px of a module centre the frame
   score is flat. `max((score, r, q))` picks the largest r, then the largest
   q, so a refined centre drifts to the +x/+y edge of that flat region,
   about +15 units. The seed is never refined, so it does not drift.

This drift is the "15 units apart" in the record. In the instrumented copy,
the up-left block of seed 0 was processed twice. The first time (reached
from the left neighbour) it landed at centre error (0, +15); the second time
(from the up neighbour) at (+15, +3).

Both arrivals inherit one axis that is still the seed's rotated axis. That
axis narrows the flat region on one image axis, so the drift goes entirely
into the other. Learning the axes as differences from neighbours that did
not drift as far then gives B = (1, 803) and A = (818, -11). Those are the
1.3-1.7 % axis errors in the record. At the far corner (-8, -8) the drift
and both axis errors add in +y, to 26-27 units. That is more than half a
module at 3 px per module (24 units), so the corner reads its neighbour's
cells.

Everything below was measured with temporary instrumented copies under
`/tmp/axes-static/` (`probe.py` writes the log and pose table, `probe5.py`
runs the variants, `run/base.py` and `run/ftmid.py` are the whole-reader
copies). The tree was not touched. Captures: the stage-1 grids
`bootstrap/build/stage1/lower/r2/page_p{0,1,2}.grid.txt`. The print of
record's `bootstrap/build/r2/page_p0.grid.txt` gives the identical result
(its page_sha256 was not checked).

## 2. Every place the four diagonals are treated differently

Ranked by what they can do to an up-left-only pattern.

**H1. ft's tie-break (line 89, `max((score, r, q))`). Necessary for the
grid misses, and a bug on exact input.**
- On a flat score it drifts the refined centre to the +x/+y plateau edge,
  about +15 units, in whichever image axis the inherited axes leave
  unconstrained.
- With ties broken to the mean of the tied candidates the misses go to zero
  on all three grids, even with the rotated seed. Blurred captures are
  unchanged.
- It is direction-asymmetric by construction: the drift is always toward
  +x/+y in image coordinates.

**H2. fm's rotation tie-break (lines 144-145,
`max((Σ|w|, r, t))` over 21 steps of 0.9 degrees). Decides WHICH class
fails.**
- On the grid Σ|w| is flat over +/-1.79 degrees, and the largest r wins.
- Forcing r moves the failing class: 0 gives L/U, +64 gives none, -128
  gives DR/UL/UR, +128 gives UL.
- The finder's own rotation is coarse (+/-0.45 degrees between steps). Only
  `reader.py` refines it, by a lattice fit and a grey ring fit.

**H3. The seed is never refined (`if a or b:` skips ft for a=b=0, line
119), yet its be centre is the base of the dist-1 blocks' learned axes.**
- The axes are differences of centres from two different estimators (be for
  the seed, ft for everything else). Any drift ft adds enters the dist-1
  axes with opposite signs on opposite sides: (0,-1) learns A - β,
  (0,+1) learns A + β, (-1,0) learns B - β, (+1,0) learns B + β.
- With rotation 0 this alone produces the L/U misses (worst error 22 units
  on L).

**H4. Inheritance of the parent's axes for ft (the push, lines 133-134).**
- Every diagonal is first predicted from its horizontal neighbour via that
  neighbour's B, and is ft-fitted with that neighbour's axes: A learned
  (carrying H3's ±β), B still the seed's.
- UL and DL share parent (0,-1); UR and DR share (0,+1). This splits left
  from right; it cannot single out UL by itself.
- Combined with H2 it decides which image axis the drift goes into (the
  rotated seed B narrows the x plateau, so the drift goes into y).

**H5. The lever arms.**
- Pose origin at module (0,0); data -8..7; frame -9 and +8, centred on
  -0.5. The far corner of UL is (-8,-8), the longest lever in both axes.
  UR's is (7,-8), DL's (-8,7), DR's (7,7).
- With learned axes, far-corner error ≈ δ + (δ - δ_nbr)·lever/17 per axis,
  with the same sign in all four quadrants (checked algebraically). So the
  lever gives UL about 8/7 more amplification than DR. It is a format
  geometry, not a bug.
- It tips marginal cases: rotation 0 gives L 7 against R 1. It cannot create
  the error.

**H6. The dedupe `w` (lines 113-115; the key is the predicted, unrefined
position floored to 5-module cells, c = 240 units on the grid) and first
prediction wins.**
- Symmetric across the four diagonals.
- A second prediction 15 units away crosses a cell edge now and then, and
  the block is processed again: UL 18 processings for 15 blocks, DL 23,
  DR 17. That gives an extra chance, never an asymmetry.
- Seed re-entries hash exactly to the seed (s + u·(seed - s)), so the seed
  is never re-processed.

**Not candidates (checked):**
- **Sign logic u and the (k>2)/(k<3) lookup (lines 122-125).** Correct for
  all four quadrants:
  - UL: A = s(-1,0) - s, B = s(0,-1) - s.
  - UR: A = s - s(-1,0), B = s(0,1) - s.
  - DL: A = s(1,0) - s, B = s - s(0,-1).
  - DR: A = s - s(1,0), B = s - s(0,1).
- **sp's floor division (line 84).** The error lies in [-16/17, 0] unit
  whatever the sign of the product: a uniform -0.5 unit offset, more than
  ten times below the effect. ax's `-17*p*r>>16` gives B0 = -26 against
  A1 = 25 (one unit).
- **The threshold `(z[8]+z[59])//2` (line 98).** Per block and
  direction-free (exactly 127 on the grid).
- **The Laplacian at the data border (line 101).** The corner cells' frame
  neighbours have the same parity for UL and DR, and the other parity for
  UR and DL. All four diagonals sit at even block distance, so they share
  the seed's flip. This can split {UL, DR} from {UR, DL}, never UL alone.
- **The edge check at -10/9 (line 116).** Only at page edges; every
  bullseye's diagonals are interior.
- **Aliasing.** Children share the parent's axis lists, but learning
  REPLACES `s[k]` and never mutates it, and `s[0]`/`s[1]` are the child's
  own. The wk locals f, u, a, b, z shadow globals, but wk calls none of
  them (rb, v and ft resolve to the globals).
- **be's first-row chord (lines 69-81).** The seed centre is taken from the
  topmost accepted row. Under shear that biases x. It is exact on the grid,
  and it is the same for all four diagonals.

## 3. What the diet changed (reader.py walk(), lines 463-530)

`reader.py` takes the axes from one global pose P, posed by a lattice fit
over all bullseyes plus their pitch. Each block carries only a translation
offset, extrapolated 2o - o_behind, and is refined by the same ft search
with the same max tie-break. Bullseye centres and pitch are refined on grey
(ring_fit).

So there the ft drift moves translations only and never enters the axes,
and there is no flat 21-step rotation search: tilt() is used only for a
lone bullseye. Learned axes (r12) are what turn a harmless translation tie
into an axis error.

## 4. Discriminating tests for the measurement log or the judge

- **T1 rotation tie.** Print fm's Σ|w| for each (r, t). Several r tied at
  the maximum means H2 is active; the chosen r is the largest of them.
  - Grid: 130050 at r = -128..128.
  - The nominal renders and the photos chose r = +64 and 0. Their tables
    were not dumped.
- **T2 ft ties.** Per block and ft level, count the candidates tied at the
  max, and log the refined centre minus the truth.
  - H1 predicts on the grid: ties at levels 2-4, every drift ≥ 0 in x and
    y, about +15 on the unconstrained axis; UL (0,15)/(15,3).
  - A physical axis error predicts no ties and drifts of both signs.
- **T3 forced rotation.** The class table in §1. Under a physical cause,
  a one-step change of an unresolvable rotation would not move the failing
  class from UL to L/U, to nothing, or to DR/UR.
- **T4 the 180-degree turn.** If the misses follow the page they stay UL
  in the page frame. Measured: DR 11, DL 9, UL 10. They follow the code's
  image-coordinate tie-break.
- **T5 the tie-break fix.** `ftmid` (the mean of the tied candidates)
  should leave every blurred capture unchanged and zero the grid.
  Measured: grids 16/17/19 → 0/0/0; nominal renders 8/5/4 → 8/5/4; photo
  chunk counts identical.
- **T6 for the photos.** H1 and H2 need exact integer ties, so on a
  blurred photo they predict NO preference for the up-left diagonal.
  - Any up-left excess the measurement agent finds on photos is a
    different mechanism.
  - Candidates for that: H3/H5 with real ft noise, or saturation plateaus
    in clipped regions of photo 1. Saturation is a possibility, not
    measured: count the ft ties on photo 1's bright side to tell.
