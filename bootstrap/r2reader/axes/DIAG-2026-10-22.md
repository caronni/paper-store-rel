# DIAG-2026-10-22 — the typed reader's unread blocks, instrumented

The question (the owner, on RECORD-2026-10-20 ~l.1210-1230): "are you sure
this is not just an algorithmic bug, given that 'left, up' and e.g. 'right,
up' or 'down, left' have similar distortions?"

Answer, measured: **it is the code.** The up-left excess appears only on
frames whose score surfaces have exactly flat maxima (the 0/1 text grid,
3 px per module; worse on the exact 600-dpi raster), and it is produced by
the reader's two `max()` tie-breaks: `fm` takes the largest rotation `r`
among exactly tied scores, `ft` takes the largest offset `(r, q)` (+x, then
+y in the image) among exactly tied scores. With a symmetric tie rule in
`ft` (a counterfactual, not the reader) every block of the grid reads; with
symmetric rules in both, the grid, the exact raster and the perfect renders
at spm 3 and 7 read every block exactly once (perfect spm 2 keeps 11
unread blocks, which do not come from ties). When the frame is turned a quarter turn, the failing
diagonal changes in the page frame, so the effect is not tied to the page.
On the nominal renders and the four phone photos there are no exact ties
in `fm` and few in `ft`, and no diagonal of a bullseye is missed more than
any other.

## What was run

- Instrument: `bootstrap/r2reader/axes/instrument.py` (driver
  `run_all.py`, run from the worktree root). It execs reader_lite_v1.py's
  own definitions (the region between `# BEGIN READER` and the main loop),
  so `ld v cb be sp ft u f a rb ax w fm` are the reader's code. Only `wk`
  is restated, statement for statement with the same integer operations,
  with a record per queue entry. `ft` is redone by `ft_trace`, which keeps
  all nine scores of each stage and is asserted equal to the reader's `ft`
  on every call. `fm` is redone by `fm_logged`, which keeps the rotation
  scores and is asserted equal to the reader's `fm`. The counterfactual
  flags `--ft-rule centre` (move to the centre of the tied candidates'
  bounding box) and `--fm-rule r0` (the tied `r` of least |r|) are the only
  departures from the reader. They are labelled `cf-*` everywhere.

  truth grid (blurred σ 0.4 module, gain and offset projected out), run
  breadth-first from the bullseyes, each block seeded by its fitted
  neighbour. A fit whose residual is over twice the frame median is
  withheld (photo 1: 8 blocks at (8-12, 10-11); photo 2: 1 block). Misread
  counts do not use the fit (block identity + truth grid only).
  Genie validated where the warp is known:

- Definitions. Classes are taken from the walk key (a, b) relative to the
  seed whose walk first processed the block: the four diagonals, axial =
  |a| + |b| = 1, other = the rest. A page block's first processed visit
  gives its class and metrics. It counts as read if any visit's CRC closed.
  Bullseye blocks and white (chunk-less) slots are excluded (photo 4
  visits 19 white slots on row 29). "far corner" = the data corner
  ((-8 | 7), (-8 | 7)) farthest from the seed: the distance between where
  the reader samples that module and the true module centre, in modules.
  "learned A/B err" = |axis - true| / |true| of the axes the block was
  read with: learned where the walk learned them, inherited otherwise.
  "misread" = the reader's 256 hard bits before CRC/flip vs the truth grid.
  Every CRC-closed block on every capture decoded its true chunk (0 wrong).
- Machine: Machine 1 (Apple M1 Max), env/bin/python (free-threaded 3.14).
  The wall times below are for the plain reader, run serially.
- JSON per capture (every visit: key, parent and direction, predicted,
  refined, inherited / learned axes and which neighbour taught each, ft's
  nine scores per stage and the tied offsets, soundness and margin, bits,
  read chunk, and the truth fields): `bootstrap/build/axes/diag/NAME.json`.

## Which diagonal, in which frame; tie counts

"by page relation" classes a block by its page offset from the nearest
bullseye block, whichever seed's walk reached it. "by image direction"
uses the step's direction in the image, from the seed's axes. "fm
rotation r (ties)" gives the chosen r (1/4096 rad steps of 64) and how many
rotation candidates tie exactly at the top score. "ft calls tied" counts
how many of `ft`'s calls had a tie at any stage / at the last (1/16
module) stage.

## Answers

**Does the up-left excess reproduce?** Yes, exactly, on the 0/1 text grid
of page 0. Stage-1 build: 16 data blocks unread (595 of 609 chunks, 14
short), 15 of them the up-left diagonal of all 15 bullseyes, 0 of the
other three diagonals. Print of record's grid: 21 unread, 15 up-left, 1
down-left, 5 other. Each up-left block is sound, has 4-7 cells misread
(mean 5.07), all 76 of those cells in its top-left (far) quadrant, and has
its far corner 0.54 module off. The other three diagonals stay at
0.40-0.46. The record's "same block reached from two neighbours landed 15
units apart" also reproduces: the up-left block refined from its left
neighbour lands at (3904, 6367), from its upper neighbour at (3919, 6355).
That is (15, -12) in 1/16 px, the same on every bullseye.

**On which captures?** Only on the text grid (both builds). It does not
appear on the perfect renders at spm 2, 3 or 7, on nominal-b030 p0/p1/p2,
on nominal spm 7, or on any photo. The exact 600-dpi raster (12 px per
module, hard edges) fails much worse: only the 60 axial neighbours read,
the other 128 visited blocks are unread (all four diagonals 15/15), and
427 blocks are never reached, because unsound blocks do not push their
neighbours.

**Perfect renders: code or physics?** The camera.py perfect renders (spm
2/3/7) show no up-left excess. At spm 2 and 3 fm has no tie (r = 0). At
spm 7 fm ties (r = +128) and every ft call ties, but the up-left far
corner stays at 0.40 module. The text grid and the exact raster are
perfect captures too, and they fail. Three tests show the failure is the
code:
1. Quarter turns move the failing diagonal in the page frame. Unturned:
   page UL 15. One turn: page UR 15 + DL 15 (image UL + DR). Two turns: UL
   10 + DL 9 + DR 11. Three turns: page UR 15 (image UL). A page-fixed
   physical cause cannot do that. The image up-left fails in all four (15,
   15, 11, 15). Why the other image diagonals that fail depend on the turn
   is not measured here. One candidate: the frame ring (-9 and +8) is
   asymmetric about the block centre and turns with the page.
2. On the grid, a symmetric `ft` tie rule alone reads all 609 chunks.
3. Symmetric rules in both `ft` and `fm` read every block exactly once
   (615 accepted, 609 chunks) on the grid, the raster, and perfect spm 3
   and 7.

**The mechanism, traced on the grid** (per-block numbers in
s1-p0-grid.json):
- `fm`'s rotation score is exactly tied for r = -128, -64, 0, 64, 128
  (130050 each). `max((score, r, t))` returns r = +128 (1.79°), so every
  seed pose is rotated (A = [815, 25], B = [-26, 815] against a true
  [816, 0], [0, 816]): 3.07 % axis error.
- Every `ft` call (662 of 662) has exactly tied stages (2-6 tied of 9 at
  the 1/4-1/16 module steps). `max((score, r, q))` moves to the largest
  tied offset, so the refined centres end near the +x/+y edge of a flat
  maximum. The landing point depends on where the search started. The
  four first-ring axial blocks land +0.04-0.08 module in y. The diagonals
  land +0.25-0.31: up-left (0.00, 0.31), up-right (0.02, 0.29), down-left
  (0.04, 0.27), down-right (0.00, 0.25).
- The learned axes are the difference of the block's refined centre and
  its placed neighbour's. So they take up the difference of the two
  plateau landing points (the record's "learned as the difference of two
  fitted positions" is the channel, not the source). The four diagonals
  get B = 803 / 806 / 827 / 824 and A tilted by -11 / +10 / -11 / +10 in
  y (1.0-1.6 %).
- All four diagonals are displaced the same way (+y). At the up-left
  block's far corner (-8, -8) the three terms add: the centre bias
  (+0.31), the shortened B (+8·13/17 units = +0.13) and the tilted A
  (+8·11/17 units = +0.11), giving 0.54 module, past the half-module line.
  At the other diagonals' far corners the terms partly cancel or are
  smaller: 0.46 / 0.46 / 0.40. So "similar distortions" holds (1.0-1.6 %
  on all four), and one diagonal crosses because of the sign.

**Dependence on samples per module.** The effect is not monotone in spm;
it follows exact ties. Perfect renders: spm 2 has 13 unread (12 other, 1
down-left; misread up to 16; cf-both still 11, so not ties; ties in 127

The learned-axis errors (mean 0.3-0.7 % on diagonals) are symmetric across
the four diagonals.

## Caveats

- Photo truth is a fitted model (the genie), validated on renders to

  counts stand, their displacement numbers are absent.
- The counterfactual rules are diagnostics, not proposed fixes. A fix
  costs typed characters and is the owner's call (RECORD ruling 29).
  `cf-fm` alone trades the 15 up-left misses for 16 axial misses on the
  grid.
- Perfect spm 2's 13 unread blocks are a separate, non-tie effect (not
  diagnosed here).
- Single renders per setting (camera seed 1). The photos are the four of
  2026-10-20.
