## 9. Search (2026-10-22, builder 2): ruling 32's rename-and-rewrite search on the new text

**What ran.** The cloud session's encoder lab
(`compressor-exploration/encoder-side/`: `names2.py`, the scope-exact
renamer; `search2.Searcher`, greedy coordinate descent over bindings;
`rewrites.py`, the equivalent spellings and top-level def moves; the C
mirror `cm2h.c` of the d() model). It is copied unchanged into
`changeset/rw/`. The driver `rw/run_search.py` replaces the lab's
`run_reader2.py` and `run_rewrites.py`. It scores in ideal cm2 bits under
**stage 1's lowercase primer** (`build/changeset/stage1/lower/a.py`
`.strip()`, orders (0,1,2,3,4,6,8)), on stage 1's typed text
(`make_typed.reader_text`). It alternates the two phases until neither
gains: renames (the lab's pool, lowercase without l plus the reader's own
two-letter names) and rewrites (greedy best-first over flip, comm, tperm,
merge, split, shift, aug and move).

The driver adds four things to the lab's loop:
- any candidate with a line over 72 characters scores +inf;
- `ld`, `wk` and `fm`, under their current names, are impure for the
  rewrites;
- no module-level binding may take a name reserved by `count_reader.py`
  (d, q, k);
- module-level renames are tracked into a name map.

Mirror check (`rw/check_mirror.py`): 12,993.6 ideal bits against 12,994
exact bits from `cm2_enc.encode_int`, which is 2,599 glyphs, builder 1's
figure. The put-back of the typed region is the identity on the
pre-search file.

**Yes, the composed reader loses what the reader of record reads, and the

sealed-seed case for the quarters.

### When the page is lost outright: the header

With one bullseye, the page header has one copy: the 68 corner modules of
that bullseye block. If it does not read, no block is read. This happened in
14 of the 32 renders, identically in all four texts:

- **The finder did not find the survivor (8 renders):**
  - n045 A, B and C, both polarities;
  - n045s2 B-white and C-ink.
- **The survivor was found but its header did not check (6 renders):**
  - n045 D and E, both polarities;
  - n045s2 A-ink and B-ink.

At sigma 0.45 the single-bullseye header read is a coin flip. On the clean
n045 page, under the pooled rotation, only 1 of the 7 found bullseyes
checks its own header: (2,14). On clean n045s2 it is 7 of 10, and on clean
n030 it is 10 of 15. (This per-bullseye check was run with the record's
definitions only.)

**The polarity of the 14 destroyed blocks decides some outcomes at blur
0.45, through the finder's global threshold.** The finder binarises the frame
at `(min(z[::97]) + max(z[::97]))//2`. When a sample of that stride lands
inside a solid blacked block, the minimum drops from 4 to 0, and the
threshold moves from 129 to 127. This happens on all three n045s2 ink
renders; the white renders and the clean render stay at 129. The survivor's
own pixels are identical in all three renders (max |diff| 0 in a 40 px
window). Yet the finder finds C at 129 and misses it at 127, finds B at 127
and misses it at 129, and reads A's header at 129 but not at 127. This is a
knife edge in the finder and header, not in the walk.

## 15. Seed ordering: every seed walked, strong seeds first (2026-10-22, one Opus measurer)

§13's leave-one-out put the union's quarter gain on the seed pass-through
alone, and its edge losses there too. The hypothesis tested here: a weak
seed's walk (a seed whose own timing-frame test fails) starts at the same
time as the strong seeds' walks, claims cells first (the walk's dedupe is
first-claim-wins) with a worse pose, and spoils blocks a strong seed's walk
would have read. So ORDER the seeds. Two texts, each the smallest edit of
the alternative's text before its search (`changeset/reader_nod_edits.py`,
sha 20f4bf4b…, items a, b, c, e, f, g, the timing test kept, no CRC/white
clause):

- **S1, "every seed walked, strong first"**: the walk passes every seed
  (`not(a or b)or` in front of the timing test, the spelling of §13's
  `both_no_crc`), and `fm` sorts the seed list by the seed's timing-frame
  score, descending, before the walk starts. The score is `abs(w(s))`: the
  walk's soundness test sums `v*(1-2*(k^s[4]))` over the frame and
  `s[4]=int(w(s)<0)`, so that sum IS `abs(w(s))` at the seed's pose (a
  seed's pose is never refined). The sort stands after the header read, so
  the header is tried in the same order as before.
- **S2, "two walks"**: the walk runs twice with the visited-cell dict
  shared. First the alternative's walk (seeds gated by the timing test);
  then a second call from every seed, with a seed passing both the gate and
  the visited check, so a seed the first walk refused walks into the cells
  the first walk left unclaimed (a seed the first walk took re-walks into
  claimed cells only: 0 such claims beyond the seed's own cell on 39
  frames). The walk copies its seed list (its queue grows in place) and
  calls itself once at its end, so the slot pass's spy, which calls the walk
  directly, sees both walks. The flag is `l`, not `u`: the walk already uses
  `u` as the axis sign in its loop; a first draft with `u` lost the flag
  there, was caught on the smoke test and rebuilt before its search.

**The arithmetic claim of F2, checked on the text.** The brief said fr adds
the offset before the //17 division. It does the opposite, and that is why
the claim holds: `fr(s,x,y) = s[0]+(s[2][0]*x+s[3][0]*y)//17, …`. The pose
position is added OUTSIDE the floor division, so fr of a pose shifted by
(r, q) is fr(s) + (r, q) exactly. `(1-2*k)*(1-2*s[4]) == 1-2*(k^s[4])` holds
for bits: k is `x+y&1`, and s[4] is `int(w(s)<0)` or `1-s[4]`. So each
candidate's score is the same integer, and the max picks the same candidate.

**The padding and its bound.** The padding is one more image of white,
r·m bytes, which is n rows (100 % of the height). An unclamped sample at
(s, p) reads the indices (p>>4)·h + (s>>4) + {0, 1, h, h+1}:

- a sample past the bottom row, or with a negative y, lands in the padding
  and reads 255 (a negative index wraps to the end of the buffer, which is
  the padding);
- an IndexError needs an overrun of n rows.

Every out-of-frame sample comes from a block or bullseye frame. The
rotation search and the header read sample at most 9 modules (plus the
bilinear neighbour) from a bullseye centre that the finder found inside
the frame. The walk samples within 9 modules (plus ft's offset, under one
module) of a block centre that the frame check keeps 10 modules inside. A found bullseye
needs its vertical comb of 13 runs inside the frame, so every overrun is
less than the frame's height, and so less than the padding.

- the deepest index past the image is 0.58 % of the image;
- the closest any frame comes to the buffer's end is 240,199 bytes, and to
