## 1. Can cm2 (a.py's d()) compress better without much more typing?

### What the record already settled

- **Orders and counters.** REV measured these on the 10-19 loader (1,116-character loader, hand payload at 64 + 3 per line), not on the current a.py (RECORDED, REV "Experiments").
  - The neighbouring counter rules (//2+1, //5+2, //8+2, //3+2) cost +2 to +12 typed.
  - Rules such as //4+1, //3+1, //2+2, //8+1 and //4+3 lose +24 to +40.
  - Adding orders 5, 10 or 16 costs +1 to +35.
- **The 12-bit clamp** is flat. REV calls the counter rule and clamp "already optimal".
- **The short tuple** (0,1,2,3,4,6) is 9 characters cheaper at the same 48 lines. It is owed to the owner's word because it changes the stage-0 spec (RECORDED, RECORD "Owed").
- **Order:** the 3-gram similarity sort and function order are flat or hurt, +2 to +11 typed (RECORDED, REV).
- **Naming:** naming is the lever. The loader's own names save 39 B over fresh names on the same code (RECORDED, REV). REV's `hand_search.py` already picked each binding's name by compressed size, with the loader as primer, through a scope-exact renamer.
- **Crinkler:** compressor-searched sparse masks and weights typed as data reach 3,863 against 3,895, which is -32 (0.8 %): +35 of decoder text against -67 of data. The masks over-fit the payload and need a 60 s re-search on every reader change. Crinkler's counter rule, x4 boost and base 10 each lose, +38 to +162 (RECORDED, CRK "The answer", §4).

**Screened and lost** (ideal bits; no round trip needed because each arm lost):
- growing boosts (+16 to +80 B ideal, monotone in K);
- boost only above an order;
- priors other than 1;
- steeper top weights;
- inheritance from the next-lower order (+7 % to +73 %; inherited counts are amplified by the shift and kill the boost);
- integer sharpening (+22 % to +60 %);
- two-rate counters;
- a column context (-2 B for +59 chars);
- a match model (+166 chars and no gain). On the reader typed twice it does work, 1.7 B against 109.2 B for the second copy, so the payloads simply lack long repeats beyond order 16;
- priming twice or with less text (per-lab figures in §4).

### What generalises, what did not

- **To the monolith and T3:**

  - Record reader: 56 glyphs (about 35 B) to reach 47 lines.

- Take the rw2 reader at the next rebuild (-106 typed, 0 decoder characters). The rebuild is already owed from ruling 29. Adoption is conditional on three things:

## 2. cmmix / strong2 with the integer tricks, and what makes strong2 better

### The big-int rANS coder in strong2 (strong2 lab)

**One-integer rANS.** The decoder spelling (form C, confirmed a correct inverse by both refuters):
```
o = x % 4096
y = o < p
x = x // 4096 * (p if y else 4096 - p) + o - p + p * y
```
- R2 bytes: monolith 10,565-10,568 against 10,599, so **-31 to -34 B** (MEASURED).
- Archive bytes at 1 MB: 255,436 against the 32-bit arithmetic coder's 255,569 (-0.05 %, MEASURED).
- **Quadratic.** Coder-only CPU is 75 / 132 / 241 µs/byte at 64K / 128K / 256K, against the arithmetic coder's 2.1 at every n (MEASURED). That projects to about 960 µs/byte at 1 MB and about 23 h of coder alone on T1 (ESTIMATE). The record had already excluded cm2 v2's coder as quadratic, 2,460 µs/byte at 1 MB EXTRAPOLATED (RECORDED, ARCH; CRK).

**Block reset** (x restarted every 2^B output bytes, 3-byte length per block, model continuous).
- Linear: blk16 coder 54.5 µs/byte at 1 MB and 51.8 at 2 MB.
- Bytes: -65 B at 1 MB, -128 B at 2 MB; blk14 +156 B.
- R2: **10,598 to 10,603**, so the R2 saving vanishes.
- A real blk16 decode of 1 MB round-trips (297 s wall under load). All MEASURED.
- Verdict: the coder swap buys nothing at the archive rung.

### Exact dicts

**strong2-exact** (nibble buckets turned into one exact-key dict):
- R2 10,458 (-141 B); with rANS 10,407 (-192 B).
- Bytes at 1 MB -27 (-0.01 %), so strong2's collisions cost almost nothing (MEASURED).

**exact2** (the match table also a dict):
- R2 10,421 (-178), with rANS 10,380 (-219).
- Bytes +293 at 1 MB, because it is an exact order-7 match.
- Peak RSS 171 MB (8K), 621 MB (256K), 1,326 MB (1 MB) (MEASURED). The growth exponent is an ESTIMATE: the lab's fit gives n^0.66, and the raw 256K→1 MB ratio gives about n^0.55. That projects to 5-11 GB on T1 (ESTIMATE).
- For comparison:
  - strong2's own RSS measured here is about 1,035-1,038 MB, fixed (MEASURED). On machine 1 it was 1.36 GB (RECORDED, ARCH).
  - The record's cm2 exact dict used 25 GB on T1 and grows with n (RECORDED, ARCH).

**A finding:** strong2's match hash `h*G>>22 & (2^26-1)` depends only on h mod 2^48, so its "order-7" match model is effectively order 6 (MEASURED by the strong2 lab; confirmed by both refuters on dec_strong2.py line 45).

### Can cmmix win the typed rung?

No.
- The cmmix-exact d() is 767 characters against 423, giving an a.py of 1,170. The payload is 1,798.0 B exact, 48 lines, **total 4,239 (+345)** (MEASURED).
- Other cmmix configs screened at 1,812-1,885 B ideal. An APM on cm2's p is at best -5 B for 100 or more characters (ESTIMATE).
- The mixer pays only at R2 size (monolith -136 B), where pages do not move.

### What makes strong2 better (archive lab, 1-2 MB prefixes of this checkout's T1)

- **CRH**, counter state cap 15 to 11, 0 characters:
  ```
  T[t] = pt << 4 | nt + (nt < 11)
  ```
  1 MB -294 B (-0.115 %), no time cost; cap 8 gives -0.137 % (MEASURED). Cap 8 measures better. No reason has been given to prefer 11.
- **AQC**, the order-3 APM keyed with the partial byte. The full line:
  ```
  a2 = (((h & 16777215) << 8 | c0) * G >> 16 & 65535) * 33 + (dd >> 7)
  ```
  - -0.036 % (MEASURED).
  - Cost: +7 B of minified decoder (r2cost.jsonl: 2,140 against 2,133, MEASURED). The edited expression itself is 8 characters longer, so the two refuters' +7 and +8 differ by counting basis. Either way it is about +4 B of R2 (ESTIMATE).
- **OL** (hashed orders 12/16/24): -0.365 %, 183 µs/byte, R2 +44 B (ESTIMATE).
- **M2** (a second match model on an order-16 hash): -0.218 %, +64 MB, R2 +153 B (ESTIMATE).
- **SP / APMM / APM2**: -0.088 / -0.081 / -0.050 %.
- **Losers** (MEASURED): order 6 +0.017 %, verified match length +0.041 %, two-layer mixer +0.774 % (one setting only), slower counters +1.05 %.
- **Finalist C1+CRH**:
  - 2 MB: 489,192 against 492,414 (**-0.654 %**), 233 µs/byte (1.67x the CRH arm's 139; 1.57x strong2's baseline 148), 1,135 MB (MEASURED). Round trip at 8 KB; C1 without CRH round-tripped at 1 MB.
  - T1: about 1,639,274 B on this tree (ESTIMATE from the MEASURED same-tree strong2 below), still **15 pages**. Decode about 20.6 min (13.1 × 233/148, ESTIMATE) and 1.46 GB (ESTIMATE).
  - Minified 2,847 B against strong2's 2,133; R2 +263 B (ESTIMATE).
- **k30 tables**: about -0.19 % on T1 at about 3.4 GB (ESTIMATE from k25 to k27 at 1 MB). That is 2.5x the record's 1.36 GB envelope, on an unknown machine.

## 3. The "perfect compressor" question

### The framing

The owner's "decoder + payload" is MDL's two-part code. The ideal limit is Kolmogorov complexity relative to Python plus the 826-character a.py: uncomputable, invariant only up to the interpreter constant, and its time-bounded forms need search exponential in program length.

PUBLISHED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_description_length ; https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/cs/9901014

What is achievable is a searched point in a small model class (masks, weights, constants, program spellings). That is what Crinkler and Roadroller do (RECORDED, CRK; SURVEY), what the record's REV hand_search did for names, and what the encoder lab extended.

### What the literature says

- **Model cost.** Déletang et al. price the model in. On 2,048-byte chunks, Chinchilla 70B is 8.3 % raw and 14,008.3 % adjusted; a 3.2M transformer is 17.0 / 17.7 %; gzip 48.1 % (PUBLISHED, https://arxiv.org/html/2309.10668v2).
- **LLM coders for code.** One LLM (DeepSeekCoder-AWQ) reports a 2.35-3.29x "gain" over zstd-22 (Table 2); other models range from 1.18x to 3.04x. The paper does not say whether weights are counted (PUBLISHED, https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24192v2). A multi-GB model is disqualified in the two-part sense either way.
- **AST / grammar coding.**
  - JSZap beats gzip by 10 % in total (PUBLISHED, https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=159070).
  - Evans and Fraser's grammar-coded bytecode reaches 29-42 % of original across both training grammars, against gzip's 31-44 % (PUBLISHED, https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~will/papers/bytecode.pdf).
    - The paper notes that the comparison favours gzip, because its own coder must stay directly interpretable.
    - Its grammar alone occupies 10,525 bytes inside the interpreter. That is direct evidence of the decoder cost.
  - Smallest-grammar search is NP-hard: Storer (1977); Charikar, Lehman, Liu, Panigrahy, Prabhakaran, Sahai and Shelat, "The smallest grammar problem", IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 51(7), 2005 (citation to be verified before use). The previously cited https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.06428 is about approximation-ratio bounds only.
  - Every scheme needs a parser in the decoder: several KB for an import-free Python parser (ESTIMATE), against a typed rung where 100 characters is a lot.
  - MEASURED here: a JSZap-style identifier-stream split loses under context mixing (+0.9 % paq8l, +3.2 % lpaq1 on the monolith; +1.0 / +2.3 % on b.py).
- **Dictionary transforms.**
  - XWRT helps weak back ends and hurts ppmonstr on book1 (+4.00 % at -f3, +0.81 % at -f20; PUBLISHED, https://mattmahoney.net/dc/dce.html §5.3.2.5).
  - book1 is a 768 KB file. The same section notes that the top 3 LTCB programs use word encoding, and that paq8hp12 and drt|lpaq9m rest on 44k-word dictionaries. So book1 does not settle the question at 9.5 MB. The archive-rung version is unmeasured (§2).
  - The preset dictionary that does pay is the primer, which the ladder already gets free: paq8l on the typed reader goes from 1,841 to 1,652 B (-10.3 %) when primed with a.py (MEASURED, difference method ±20 B).
- **Compression-aware minification.** There is partial prior art:
  - UglifyJS mangles names by character frequency for gzip (PUBLISHED, https://lisperator.net/uglifyjs/mangle).
  - RegPack renames variables to free tokens for its packer, and reverts method renaming when the packed output gets longer (PUBLISHED, https://github.com/Siorki/RegPack).
  - Crinkler searches section order (RECORDED, SURVEY).
  - The record's REV hand_search chose names by compressed size under cm2.
